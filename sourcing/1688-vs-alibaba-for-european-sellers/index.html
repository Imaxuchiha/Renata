1688 vs Alibaba for European sellers: what actually changes?

The core difference is that 1688 is China's domestic wholesale market, Chinese only, with no English middle layer, while Alibaba is built for export and speaks English. That single fact changes both the price you see and the risk you carry. Here is what it means in practice for a European seller.

By Renata · September 29, 2026 · 6 min

What 1688 and Alibaba actually are

Alibaba.com is an export platform. Sellers there expect foreign buyers, list prices in USD or EUR, and often have English-speaking staff or at least a translation layer. 1688.com is the opposite: it is the domestic Chinese wholesale market, where Chinese businesses buy from Chinese factories. The interface, the listings, the chat, the payment and the after-sales process all assume you are in China and speak Chinese.

That is not a small detail. On 1688 you are not a foreign buyer being served by an export desk. You are a user of a domestic system, and the system does not adapt to you. On Alibaba you are the target customer. On 1688 you are a guest who has to figure out the rules.

  • Alibaba: export-oriented, English available, international payment and shipping options.
  • 1688: domestic Chinese market, Chinese only, local payment and logistics norms.
  • The gap is not just language. It is who the platform is designed to protect and serve.

Why 1688 prices look lower, and what that really means

Prices on 1688 are usually quoted in RMB and reflect domestic wholesale rates. They often look lower than Alibaba listings for similar-looking items. But that difference is not free money. It reflects the absence of an export layer: no English-speaking sales staff, no export documentation support, no international payment handling, no built-in assumption that the buyer is abroad.

When you buy from 1688 as a European, you are essentially trying to act like a Chinese domestic buyer. You need a way to pay in RMB, a Chinese address or a forwarder, and enough Chinese to confirm details. The lower headline price is real, but so is the work and risk you take on to access it.

The language barrier is not just translation

On 1688, product titles, specifications and chat are in Chinese. Machine translation helps with simple listings, but it fails on the things that matter: material grades, tolerances, MOQ conditions, lead times, and the difference between what a supplier says and what they mean. A phrase like 起订量 (qǐ dìng liàng) means minimum order quantity, but the real question is whether that number is fixed or negotiable, and that answer often comes in a roundabout way.

  • Machine translation handles product titles, not negotiation nuance.
  • Supplier answers often imply conditions they do not state directly.
  • HSK 4 covers operational business Chinese, not contracts or technical specs.

Risk shifts to you on 1688

On Alibaba, if something goes wrong, there is a platform dispute process, Trade Assurance in many cases, and a seller who expects foreign buyers to complain. On 1688, the dispute process is domestic and in Chinese. You are not the customer the system is designed to protect. If a supplier sends the wrong item or refuses a refund, your options are limited and slow.

That does not mean 1688 is a trap. It means the risk is real and you should price it in. For small test orders, the savings can be worth it. For larger orders, the lack of recourse can wipe out the margin advantage. The question is not which platform is cheaper. It is whether you can carry the risk that comes with the cheaper one.

How European sellers actually use both

A common pattern is to use Alibaba for first orders and supplier discovery, because the English layer and platform support reduce early risk. Once you know the product, the specs and the supplier type, you can look at 1688 for the same or similar items and compare real landed cost, not just headline price. Some sellers use a sourcing agent or forwarder who buys on 1688 on their behalf.

What to do before you choose

Before you commit to 1688, answer three questions honestly. Can you pay in RMB without excessive fees? Do you have a forwarder or agent who can receive and ship domestic goods? Can you communicate well enough in Chinese to confirm specs, MOQ and lead times? If any answer is no, Alibaba is the lower-risk starting point, even at a higher price.

If you want to try 1688 but the language is the blocker, that is exactly the gap I fill. I do not guarantee suppliers or outcomes. I help you ask the right questions and understand the answers. That reduces the chance of a costly misunderstanding, but it does not remove the risk of buying from a domestic Chinese platform as a foreigner.

Common questions

Is 1688 cheaper than Alibaba for European sellers?

Headline prices on 1688 are often lower because you are seeing domestic Chinese wholesale rates without an export layer. But the real cost includes payment in RMB, a forwarder, and the risk of limited recourse if something goes wrong. Compare landed cost and risk, not just the listed price.

Can I use 1688 if I do not speak Chinese?

You can browse with machine translation, but the important parts, like specifications, MOQ conditions and lead times, are easy to misread. Supplier chat is in Chinese and often implies conditions rather than stating them. Without Chinese support, you are guessing on the details that decide whether an order works.

What does 1688 mean in English?

1688 (yī liù bā bā) is the name of the platform, Alibaba's domestic Chinese wholesale site. It is not an acronym. The name is just the number, chosen for the sound. In practice it means the Chinese domestic market, not an export marketplace.
