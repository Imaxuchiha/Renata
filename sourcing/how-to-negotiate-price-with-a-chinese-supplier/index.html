Negotiation guide

How do you negotiate price with a Chinese supplier without stalling the deal?

You negotiate price with a Chinese supplier by trading something they value, not by asking for a discount. Volume, lead time and specification are your three levers. Asking for a lower price outright usually stalls the conversation because it gives the supplier nothing to work with.

By Renata · October 10, 2026 · 5 min

Why asking for a discount outright usually stalls

When you open with a request for a lower price, the supplier hears a demand with no justification. In Chinese business culture, direct confrontation is often avoided. Instead of saying no, the supplier may go quiet, repeat the original quote, or say they will check with the manager. The conversation loses momentum.

A better approach is to offer a trade. You are not asking for a favour; you are proposing a different deal. That gives the supplier a reason to move and keeps the relationship constructive.

  • Volume: a larger order or a longer commitment
  • Lead time: a longer production window or flexible delivery
  • Specification: simpler packaging, fewer colours, standard materials

Trade on volume: how to ask without overcommitting

Volume is the most common lever, but you must be careful. Do not promise a quantity you cannot buy. Instead, ask for a price at a higher quantity and explain that you are testing the market. For example: 如果订5000件，价格是多少？ (Rúguǒ dìng wǔqiān jiàn, jiàgé shì duōshǎo?), If we order 5,000 pieces, what is the price?

You can also ask about a阶梯价格 (jiētī jiàgé), tiered pricing. This shows you are serious about scaling and gives the supplier a reason to quote lower. But never commit to a volume you cannot afford. A broken promise damages trust more than a low initial order.

Trade on lead time: give them flexibility, get a better price

Factories plan production around orders. If you can wait longer, the supplier can fit your order into a slower period, reducing overtime or expedited costs. Ask: 如果我们不急着要，可以便宜一点吗？ (Rúguǒ wǒmen bù jízhe yào, kěyǐ piányi yīdiǎn ma?), If we are not in a hurry, can it be cheaper?

This works especially well if your product is not seasonal. A longer lead time might mean the supplier uses slower shipping or produces during off-peak hours. You get a lower price; they get smoother scheduling.

Trade on specification: simplify to reduce cost

Specification changes are often the easiest way to lower the price without hurting the supplier's margin. Ask what can be simplified: packaging, colours, materials, or accessories. For example: 如果包装简单一点，价格可以低多少？ (Rúguǒ bāozhuāng jiǎndān yīdiǎn, jiàgé kěyǐ dī duōshǎo?), If the packaging is simpler, how much lower can the price be?

Be careful not to compromise on quality where it matters. But often, small changes like removing a logo from the box or using a standard colour can reduce costs. The supplier may also suggest alternatives you had not considered.

  • Standard packaging instead of custom
  • Fewer colour options
  • Slightly different material that meets your needs
  • No logo printing or simpler printing

How to phrase your request in Chinese

Tone matters. Avoid direct demands like 太贵了 (tài guì le), too expensive. That can sound like a complaint. Instead, use conditional sentences: 如果...可以...吗？ (Rúguǒ... kěyǐ... ma?), If..., can you...? This softens the request and invites a counter-offer.

Another useful phrase is 我们能不能一起想办法？ (Wǒmen néng bù néng yīqǐ xiǎng bànfǎ?), Can we find a solution together? It signals partnership rather than confrontation. If you are not confident in Chinese, you can use English, but simple Chinese often builds goodwill.

When to walk away and when to accept

Not every negotiation ends with a lower price. If the supplier will not move on price, ask about other terms: payment schedule, sample costs, or shipping. Sometimes the total cost can be reduced even if the unit price stays the same.

If the supplier is firm and you still want to work with them, accept gracefully. A good relationship is worth more than a few cents per unit. But if the price is far above your budget and they will not budge, it is okay to say you will keep them in mind for future projects.

Common questions

What is the best way to start a price negotiation with a Chinese supplier?

Start by asking about volume pricing or lead time flexibility, not by asking for a discount. For example, ask for a quote at a higher quantity or say you can accept a longer lead time. This gives the supplier a reason to adjust the price.

Should I mention my target price first?

It is usually better to let the supplier quote first. If you mention a very low target price, they may assume you are not serious or reduce quality to meet it. Instead, ask what is possible under different conditions.

How do I say 'too expensive' in Chinese without being rude?

Avoid saying 太贵了 (tài guì le) directly. Instead, say something like 价格有点高，我们能不能商量一下？ (Jiàgé yǒudiǎn gāo, wǒmen néng bù néng shāngliáng yīxià?), The price is a bit high, can we discuss it? This is softer and more constructive.

What if the supplier won't lower the price at all?

Ask about other terms: payment schedule, sample costs, or shipping. Sometimes you can reduce total cost without changing the unit price. If not, decide if the supplier is still worth it for quality or reliability.

Can I negotiate price after placing a sample order?

Yes, but it is better to negotiate before production. After a sample order, you have a relationship and can discuss larger quantities. However, if you already agreed on a price, renegotiating can damage trust.
