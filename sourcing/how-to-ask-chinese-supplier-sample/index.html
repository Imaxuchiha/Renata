Supplier samples

How to ask a Chinese supplier for a sample?

To ask a Chinese supplier for a sample, send a short message that names the exact product, asks for the sample cost and shipping cost separately, and requests a photo of the actual sample before it ships. Keep it polite, specific and in writing. This page shows you who pays for what, what to confirm, and the sentence that stops a different product arriving.

By Renata · October 6, 2026 · 7 min

Who pays for the sample and the shipping?

In most cases, you pay for the sample itself and you pay for the shipping. Suppliers rarely send free samples to new buyers, because they do not know you yet and the cost is real. Sometimes a supplier will refund the sample fee later if you place a bulk order, but that is not guaranteed and you should not assume it.

Shipping is usually the bigger surprise. A small sample can cost more to ship than the product itself, especially if it is sent by express courier. Ask for the shipping cost separately from the sample cost, so you can see both numbers clearly. If the supplier says the sample is free, ask who pays the courier.

You should also ask about the sample being retail-ready or just a production piece. A retail-ready sample may cost more because it includes packaging and labels. A production piece is often cheaper but may not show the final look.

  • Sample cost: usually paid by you, sometimes refundable on a bulk order.
  • Shipping cost: usually paid by you, and often higher than the sample price.
  • Packaging: ask if the sample includes retail packaging or not.

What to confirm in writing before you pay

Before you send any money, confirm the exact product in writing. That means the model number, size, colour, material and any logo or label. If you only discussed it in a video call, send a short message that repeats what you agreed. This protects you if the wrong item arrives.

Ask for the sample cost, the shipping cost and the total in one message. Ask for the supplier's payment details in the same message. If the supplier uses a payment method you do not recognise, ask for the company name and bank account name. They should match the company you are talking to.

Also confirm the delivery time. Ask when the sample will be sent and how long the courier usually takes. If the supplier says 'soon', ask for a specific number of days. A written date is easier to follow up on.

  • Product details: model, size, colour, material, logo.
  • Costs: sample cost, shipping cost, total.
  • Payment: company name and bank account name.
  • Timing: dispatch date and estimated delivery days.

The sentence that stops a different product arriving

The sentence that stops a different product arriving is a request for a photo of the actual sample before it is shipped. In Chinese, you can write: 请先发一张样品的照片给我确认，然后再寄出。 (Qǐng xiān fā yī zhāng yàngpǐn de zhàopiàn gěi wǒ quèrèn, ránhòu zài jì chū.) This means: Please send me a photo of the sample for confirmation first, then send it out.

This works because it forces the supplier to show you the real item, not a catalogue image. If the photo shows the wrong colour or model, you can correct it before the sample leaves China. It also creates a written record of what you approved.

If the supplier refuses to send a photo, treat that as a warning sign. A serious supplier can usually take a phone photo in a few minutes. You can also ask for a short video if a photo is not clear enough.

How to write the first message

Start with a greeting and say who you are. For example: 你好，我是[你的名字]，来自[你的公司]。 (Nǐ hǎo, wǒ shì [your name], láizì [your company].) This means: Hello, I am [your name] from [your company]. Then ask about the sample directly.

Keep the message short. Chinese suppliers are busy and they read many messages. Use simple sentences and avoid long paragraphs. If you can, use a translation tool for the first draft, but check the key words yourself or ask someone who reads Chinese.

If you do not read Chinese, you can write in English. Many suppliers can read basic English. But if you want to be sure, send both English and Chinese. That way there is less chance of a misunderstanding.

  • Greet and introduce yourself.
  • Name the exact product.
  • Ask for sample cost and shipping cost.
  • Ask for a photo before shipping.

What to do when the sample arrives

When the sample arrives, check it against what you confirmed in writing. Look at the colour, size, material and any logo. If something is wrong, take clear photos and send them to the supplier with a short message. Ask what they can do to fix it.

If the sample is correct, tell the supplier clearly. Then ask about the bulk order price, the minimum order quantity and the production time. Keep all of this in writing, because you will need it later if you place an order.

Do not assume that a good sample means every future order will be the same. A sample is one piece. Production can vary. If you order in bulk, you may want to discuss quality checks with the supplier or with a third party. That is outside what I do, but it is part of the normal import process.
