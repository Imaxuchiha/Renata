Supplier trust check

How to tell if a Chinese supplier is trustworthy

You cannot be sure a Chinese supplier is trustworthy, but you can check verifiable signals on the platform and in a short video call. Look for consistency between what they claim and what you can see. If small things do not match, bigger things probably will not either. Trust is built from evidence, not from a good feeling.

By Renata · October 2, 2026 · 6 min

Start with the platform profile, not the chat

Before you message anyone, open their profile on the platform you found them on. Check how long they have been there, whether they have a verified business badge, and what their response rate looks like. These are not guarantees, but they are harder to fake than a friendly message.

Read the product listings carefully. A trustworthy supplier usually has consistent photos, clear specifications, and a real business scope. If the same photos appear on five different stores, or the descriptions are copied from a big brand, treat that as a warning.

  • Years active on the platform
  • Verified business status (not just a personal account)
  • Response rate and response time
  • Whether listings match their stated main products

What a five-minute video call reveals

Ask for a short video call. You do not need a full factory tour. In five minutes you can see if they are in an office, a workshop, or a warehouse, and whether they can show you the product you are asking about. A supplier who refuses any video call, or keeps postponing, is telling you something.

During the call, ask them to show the product from different angles. Ask a simple question about materials or packaging. If they cannot answer without checking with someone else, that is normal for a trading company, but they should say so clearly instead of guessing.

  • They appear in a real workspace, not a blank wall
  • They can show the product you asked about
  • They answer basic questions directly or say they will check
  • They do not pressure you to pay immediately

Red flags in writing and in payment requests

Watch how they write. If every message is a copy-paste template, or if they avoid answering specific questions, that is a sign. A trustworthy supplier will confirm details in writing, even if their English is not perfect. You can also ask for their business license (营业执照, yíngyè zhízhào, business license) and check the company name matches the bank account.

Payment requests are a common trap. If they ask you to pay to a personal account instead of a company account, or if the bank account name does not match the company name, stop. Legitimate suppliers can explain why the account name is different, but it should be a clear, documented reason.

Questions to ask before you pay a deposit

Ask for the full company name in Chinese and English, and the address. Then ask them to show the business license on a video call. You can check the name against the bank account before sending any money. If they hesitate, that is your answer.

Ask about lead times, packaging, and what happens if the product does not match the sample. A trustworthy supplier will have clear answers, even if they are not what you want to hear. A supplier who promises everything and anything is usually promising nothing.

  • What is your full company name and business license number?
  • Can you show the license on a video call?
  • Is the bank account in the same company name?
  • What is the lead time for this exact product?
  • What happens if the goods do not match the sample?

When to walk away

Walk away if they refuse a video call, if the payment account does not match the company, or if they pressure you to pay outside the platform. Also walk away if they cannot answer basic questions about their own product without guessing. These are not small details; they are the foundation of the deal.

Trust your own checks, not just a good feeling. A supplier who is real will not mind you verifying. A supplier who is not will try to rush you. You do not need to be an expert in Chinese business to spot the difference.

Common questions

Can I trust a Chinese supplier just because they have a verified badge?

No. A verified badge means the platform checked some business documents, but it does not guarantee quality, delivery or honesty. Use it as one signal among many. Always check the business license name against the bank account and ask for a short video call.

What should I look for in a five-minute video call?

Look for a real workspace, the product you asked about, and direct answers to simple questions. If they cannot show the product or keep avoiding the call, that is a warning. You do not need a long tour; five minutes is enough to see if they are real.

Is it safe to pay a Chinese supplier by bank transfer?

It can be safe if the bank account name matches the company name on the business license. If they ask you to pay to a personal account or a different company name, stop and ask why. A legitimate supplier can explain, but the reason should be documented.

What does 营业执照 (yíngyè zhízhào) mean?

It means business license. It is the official document that shows a company is registered in China. You can ask for a photo or a video call showing it. Check that the company name matches the name on the bank account before you pay.
