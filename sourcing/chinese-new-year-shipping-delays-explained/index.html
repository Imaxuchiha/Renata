Chinese New Year Shipping Delays Explained

Chinese New Year shipping delays happen because factories close for two to four weeks, not just a few days. The entire supply chain—from production to freight—slows down or stops. If you plan backwards from your target delivery date and ask the right questions in November, you can avoid the worst of it.

By Renata · August 19, 2026 · 6 min

Why Chinese New Year Stops Everything

Chinese New Year (春节, chūnjié, Spring Festival) is the most important holiday in China. Workers travel home to be with family, and factories shut down completely. This is not a short break: most production lines stop for at least two weeks, and many for a month or more.

The shutdown affects more than just the factory floor. Suppliers stop taking new orders, raw material deliveries halt, and logistics providers reduce staff. Even after the factory reopens, it takes days to restart production and catch up on backlogged orders.

  • Factories close 1-2 weeks before the holiday and reopen 1-2 weeks after.
  • Freight forwarders and ports operate with reduced staff.
  • Production capacity is fully booked for weeks before the holiday.

The Real Timeline: Weeks, Not Days

Many buyers assume a few days of closure, but the reality is longer. The official holiday is usually 7 days, but most factories extend it to 2-3 weeks because workers need travel time and many take extra leave. Add the pre-holiday rush and post-holiday ramp-up, and you are looking at 4-6 weeks of reduced output.

For example, if Chinese New Year falls in late January, you should assume no production from mid-January to mid-February. If you order in December, your goods may not ship until March. That is why planning backwards from your target date is critical.

  • Official holiday: 7 days (usually).
  • Actual factory closure: 2-4 weeks.
  • Full recovery to normal output: up to 6 weeks.

What to Ask Your Supplier in November

November is the time to ask specific questions, not just 'when is your holiday?' You need to know exact closure dates, last order dates, and production lead times. Ask in writing and get answers in writing.

Here are the questions to ask:

What are your exact closure dates? (春节放假日期, chūnjié fàngjià rìqī, Chinese New Year holiday dates)

What is the last day to place an order before the holiday? (最晚下单日期, zuìwǎn xiàdān rìqī, latest order date)

What is the production lead time for my product? (生产周期, shēngchǎn zhōuqī, production cycle)

Will you have staff available during the holiday for urgent questions? (假期有值班人员吗, jiàqī yǒu zhíbān rényuán ma, are there duty staff during the holiday?)

What is your shipping cutoff before the holiday? (节前发货截止日期, jié qián fāhuò jiézhǐ rìqī, pre-holiday shipping deadline)

  • Ask for exact dates, not just 'we close for a week'.
  • Get written confirmation of lead times.
  • Clarify what happens if your order is late.

Plan Backwards from Your Target Date

Start with the date you need the goods in your warehouse or at your customer's door. Work backwards: add shipping time (ocean or air), customs clearance, and production time. Then add a buffer of at least 2 weeks for Chinese New Year disruption.

For example, if you need goods by March 1, and ocean shipping takes 30 days, production takes 20 days, and customs takes 5 days, you need to start production by late January. That means you must place your order in early January at the latest—but many factories stop taking orders in December. So you actually need to order in November.

This is why November is the critical month. If you wait until December, you may be too late.

What If You Miss the Window?

If you miss the pre-holiday ordering window, you have a few options. You can wait until after the holiday, but that means delays of 4-6 weeks. You can pay for air freight instead of ocean, but that is much more expensive. Or you can find a supplier that stays open, but that is rare and often unreliable.

The best strategy is to avoid the problem entirely by planning ahead. If you are already in a bind, be honest with your supplier and ask for realistic timelines. Do not expect miracles.

Common questions

How long do Chinese factories close for Chinese New Year?

Most factories close for 2 to 4 weeks, not just the official 7-day holiday. Workers need travel time and often take extra leave, so production stops well before and after the holiday. Plan for at least a month of reduced output.

When should I place my order to avoid Chinese New Year delays?

Place your order in November, at the latest early December. Factories stop taking new orders 2-4 weeks before the holiday, and production slots fill up fast. Ordering in November gives you time to confirm details and secure a production slot before the shutdown.

What questions should I ask my supplier about Chinese New Year?

Ask for exact closure dates, the last day to place an order, production lead time, shipping cutoff, and whether any staff will be available during the holiday. Get all answers in writing to avoid misunderstandings.

Can I still order in December and get goods before Chinese New Year?

It is risky. Many factories stop accepting orders in December because they are already at full capacity. Even if you order, production may not start until after the holiday. You might get lucky with air freight, but do not count on it.

What is the difference between the official holiday and actual factory closure?

The official holiday is usually 7 days, but factories often close longer. Workers travel home and return slowly, so production may not resume fully for 2-4 weeks. Always ask for the actual closure dates, not the official ones.
