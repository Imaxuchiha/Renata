Brazilian Portuguese basics

What does oi mean in Brazilian Portuguese, and how do you reply?

Oi means hello in Brazilian Portuguese. It is the greeting Brazilians use most in everyday speech, with friends, family, colleagues and shop staff. It is informal but not rude, and it works at almost any time of day. This page explains what oi means, how to pronounce it, when to use olá instead, and how to answer it naturally.

By Renata · November 14, 2026 · 5 min

Oi: the short answer

Oi is a greeting. It is the Brazilian equivalent of hi, and it is what you will hear most often in Brazil when people meet. It is not slang, and it is not regional: you hear it in São Paulo, Rio, Salvador and Porto Alegre alike. It is simply the normal, friendly way to say hello.

It is informal, but that does not mean it is impolite. You can say oi to a colleague, a neighbour, a barista or a friend's parents. What you would not do is open a formal letter or a job interview with oi. In those situations, use olá, which is neutral and slightly more formal.

  • Oi = hi / hello (informal, everyday)
  • Olá = hello (neutral, slightly more formal)
  • Oi, tudo bem? = hi, how are you? (the most common greeting in Brazil)

How to pronounce oi

Oi is one syllable and sounds close to the English word oh-ee said quickly, with the stress on the o. The o is open, like the o in the English word hot, and the i is short, like the i in see. Do not pronounce it as the English word oy, as in boy.

Brazilians often say it twice: oi, oi. That is friendly and casual, not a different word. You will also hear oi followed by a name, as in Oi, Ana, or by tudo bem, as in Oi, tudo bem?

Oi, olá, and other greetings

Brazilian Portuguese has several greetings, and the choice depends on how formal the situation is. Oi is the most common in speech. Olá is the safe formal option, and it is also what you often see in writing. Bom dia, boa tarde and boa noite are time-based greetings and work in any register.

One thing to watch: in European Portuguese, oi is used much less as a greeting and can sound odd or even rude in some contexts. If you learned Portuguese from a European course, you may have been taught olá as the default. In Brazil, oi is the default in everyday life.

How to reply to oi

The natural reply to oi is oi back, usually followed by tudo bem? You can also answer with olá if you want to sound a little more formal, or with a time-based greeting such as bom dia. What you should not do is stay silent or answer with a long sentence: oi is a quick, friendly exchange.

If someone says oi, tudo bem?, the expected reply is tudo bem, e você? or tudo bom, e você?. Both are correct in Brazil. Tudo bem is the more common form in speech, and tudo bom is also heard, especially in São Paulo and the South.

  • Oi, tudo bem? → Oi, tudo bem, e você?
  • Oi! → Oi! / Olá!
  • E aí? → E aí, tudo bem?

Oi in writing and online

In WhatsApp messages, oi is everywhere. Brazilians often open with oi, tudo bem? and then go straight to the point. You will also see oi written as oi or oie, which is a slightly more affectionate, playful spelling. Oie is not wrong, but it is informal and mostly used between friends.

In email, oi is fine with colleagues you know well. For a first contact with a company or an official institution, use olá or bom dia instead. That is the same rule as in speech: oi is friendly, olá is neutral, and the time-based greetings are always safe.

Common questions

Is oi formal or informal in Brazilian Portuguese?

Oi is informal, but it is not rude. It is the normal greeting between friends, family, colleagues and even shop staff. For formal situations, such as a job interview or a first email to a company, use olá or bom dia instead.

What is the difference between oi and olá?

Oi means hi and is the everyday greeting in Brazil. Olá means hello and is neutral or slightly more formal. Both are correct, but Brazilians use oi far more often in speech. In writing, olá is safer when you do not know the person.

How do you pronounce oi in Brazilian Portuguese?

Oi sounds like oh-ee said quickly, with an open o as in the English word hot and a short i as in see. Do not pronounce it like the English word oy. Brazilians often say it twice, oi, oi, which is friendly and casual.

How do you reply to oi, tudo bem?

The natural reply is oi, tudo bem, e você? or oi, tudo bom, e você? Both are correct in Brazil. Tudo bem is more common in speech, and tudo bom is also heard, especially in São Paulo and the South.

Is oi used in European Portuguese?

Oi exists in European Portuguese, but it is used much less as a greeting and can sound odd or even rude in some contexts. In Brazil, oi is the default everyday greeting. If you learned European Portuguese, you may have been taught olá as the main greeting, which is not how Brazilians usually speak.

Can I use oi in a work email in Brazil?

Yes, with colleagues you know well. For a first contact with a company or an official institution, use olá or bom dia instead. Oi is friendly and informal, so it fits internal messages better than formal correspondence.
