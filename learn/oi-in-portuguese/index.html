What does oi mean in Portuguese?

Oi means hi in Brazilian Portuguese. It is the most common everyday greeting in Brazil, used in person, on the phone, and in messages. It is informal but polite enough for almost any situation. If you are learning Portuguese, oi is one of the first words you should feel comfortable using.

By Renata · September 29, 2026 · 5 min

Oi is Brazilian Portuguese for hi

In Brazil, oi is the standard informal greeting. You say it when you meet someone, when you answer the phone, and when you start a WhatsApp message. It is warm and neutral, not slangy, and it works with friends, family, coworkers, and shop staff.

The pronunciation is simple: oi sounds like the English word oy. The o is open, and the i is short. In European Portuguese, oi is not the normal greeting. If you use oi in Lisbon, people will understand you, but it sounds Brazilian.

  • Oi, tudo bem? Hi, how are you?
  • Oi, bom dia! Hi, good morning!
  • Oi, tudo bom? Hi, all good? (very common in São Paulo)

Oi vs olá: which one should you use?

Olá is also hello, but it is more formal and more written than spoken. In everyday Brazilian conversation, oi is far more common. Olá can sound a little stiff in a casual setting, though it is perfectly correct.

Use oi with people you know or in relaxed situations. Use olá in emails, presentations, or when you want to sound neutral and polite. Both are safe, but oi is the one you will hear constantly in Brazil.

How to reply to oi

The most natural reply is oi back, often followed by tudo bem? or tudo bom? That means hi, how are you? The other person will usually answer tudo bem or tudo, and then the conversation starts.

If you want to be a little more formal, you can reply with olá or bom dia. But in Brazil, replying oi, tudo bem? is almost always the right move. It keeps the tone friendly and matches what the other person said.

  • Oi, tudo bem? Hi, how are you?
  • Tudo bem, e você? All good, and you?
  • Tudo bom, obrigado. All good, thanks. (said by a man)
  • Tudo bom, obrigada. All good, thanks. (said by a woman)

Oi is not European Portuguese

This is the part most websites get wrong. In Portugal, the normal greeting is olá, bom dia, boa tarde, or boa noite. Oi exists in European Portuguese, but it is used differently and can sound odd or even rude in some contexts. It is not the friendly hi that Brazilians use.

If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, learn oi as your default greeting. If you are learning European Portuguese, do not use oi the same way. The two varieties are different enough that mixing them sounds strange to native speakers on both sides.

Other meanings of oi in Brazil

Oi can also mean oops or excuse me when you did not hear something. If someone says oi? with a rising intonation, they are asking you to repeat what you said. It is like saying sorry? or what? in English.

In some regions, oi is used to get attention, similar to hey. You might hear oi, moço! to call a waiter or oi, psiu! to get someone's attention. Context and tone decide the meaning, but the friendly greeting is by far the most common.

  • Oi? Sorry, what did you say?
  • Oi, moço! Hey, sir! (to call a waiter)
  • Oi, psiu! Hey, psst! (to get attention)

Common questions

What does oi mean in Portuguese?

Oi means hi in Brazilian Portuguese. It is the most common informal greeting in Brazil, used in person, on the phone, and in messages. It is friendly and neutral, not slang.

Is oi the same in Portugal?

No. In European Portuguese, the normal greeting is olá, bom dia, boa tarde, or boa noite. Oi exists but is used differently and can sound odd or rude in some situations. If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, use oi. If you are learning European Portuguese, do not use it the same way.

How do I reply to oi?

The most natural reply is oi, tudo bem? That means hi, how are you? The other person will usually answer tudo bem or tudo, and then you continue. You can also reply with olá or bom dia if you want to be a little more formal.

Is oi formal or informal?

Oi is informal but polite. It is fine with friends, family, coworkers, and shop staff. For very formal situations, like a job interview or a formal email, olá is safer.

Can oi mean something else?

Yes. Oi? with a rising intonation means sorry? or what did you say? It asks the other person to repeat. In some regions, oi is also used to get attention, like hey.
