Brazilian Portuguese basics

What does oi mean in Portuguese to English?

Oi means hi in English. It is the everyday Brazilian Portuguese greeting you use with friends, family, colleagues and shop staff. It is informal but not rude, and it is far more common in Brazil than olá. This page explains how to use it, how to answer it, and the mistakes English speakers make with it.

By Renata · November 19, 2026 · 5 min

Oi is hi, not hello

Oi is the standard informal greeting in Brazilian Portuguese. You say it when you meet someone, when you answer the phone, and when you start a message. It is short, friendly and neutral in tone. English speakers often translate it as hello, but hi is closer.

Olá also means hello, but it sounds more formal or written in Brazil. You will see olá in emails, notices and speeches. In conversation, Brazilians say oi almost every time. If you are unsure which one to use with a new person, oi is still safe in most everyday situations.

  • Oi, tudo bem? Hi, how are you?
  • Oi, bom dia! Hi, good morning!
  • Oi, gente! Hi, everyone! (informal, group)

How to reply to oi

The natural reply is another oi, often followed by tudo bem? (literally all good?). You can answer tudo bem or tudo bom, both meaning I am fine. Then you can ask e você? (and you?). This short exchange is the normal opening of almost every Brazilian conversation.

If someone says oi to you, you do not need to say olá back. That would sound stiff. Repeat oi, or use oi, tudo bem? to keep the same friendly register. In a work context, you can add bom dia or boa tarde after the oi.

Oi is Brazilian, not European

In Portugal, people say olá far more often, and oi can sound odd or even rude depending on the region and the person. Brazilian Portuguese uses oi constantly; European Portuguese does not. This is one of the first differences learners notice.

The pronunciation also differs. In Brazil, oi sounds like oh-ee, with two clear vowel sounds. In Portugal, you may hear a shorter, flatter version. If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, copy the Brazilian pronunciation and use oi freely.

Common mistakes English speakers make

The biggest mistake is using oi in a formal email or with a much older person you have never met. In those cases, use bom dia, boa tarde or boa noite, or olá if you want a neutral written greeting. Oi is for people you already know or for casual service situations.

Another mistake is translating oi as hey. Hey exists in Brazilian Portuguese too, but it is more attention-getting and can sound impatient. Oi is softer and more common. Also, do not confuse oi with oi? said with a rising tone, which can mean excuse me? or come again? in some contexts.

  • Use oi with friends, family, colleagues and shop staff.
  • Use bom dia, boa tarde or boa noite in formal or first-contact situations.
  • Do not use oi in a job application or a formal email.
  • Do not translate oi as hey in every case.

Other meanings of oi

Oi can also be an interjection meaning oops or oh, especially when something goes wrong. For example, if you drop something, you might say oi. The meaning depends on tone and context. In writing, the greeting is far more common.

There is also the expression dar um oi, which means to say hi or to greet someone briefly. For example, ele deu um oi e saiu (he said hi and left). This is useful to know because it shows oi working as a noun in everyday speech.

Learn oi in context

If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, oi is one of the first words to master. Practise it with the reply tudo bem and the follow-up e você. Say it out loud until the two vowels feel natural. Then use it in real conversations, even short ones.

Common questions

Is oi formal or informal in Portuguese?

Oi is informal. It is the normal greeting among friends, family, colleagues and in casual service situations. For formal or written contexts, use olá or a time-of-day greeting like bom dia.

What is the difference between oi and olá?

Both mean hello, but oi is informal and extremely common in Brazil. Olá is more formal or written and is used more in Portugal. In everyday Brazilian conversation, oi is the default.

How do you pronounce oi in Brazilian Portuguese?

In Brazil, oi sounds like oh-ee, with two clear vowel sounds. The o is open and the i is short. Avoid reducing it to a single sound, which is more typical of European Portuguese.

Can I use oi in Portugal?

You can, but it may sound unusual or too informal in Portugal, where olá is more common. If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, use oi as Brazilians do, but be aware of the difference when you travel.

What does tudo bem mean after oi?

Tudo bem literally means all good. After oi, it works like how are you? The usual answer is tudo bem or tudo bom, often followed by e você? (and you?).

Does oi have other meanings?

Yes. Oi can be an interjection like oops or oh when something happens. It also appears in the expression dar um oi, meaning to say hi briefly. Context and tone make the meaning clear.
