Brazilian Portuguese basics

What are the days of the week in Brazilian Portuguese?

The days of the week in Brazilian Portuguese are domingo, segunda-feira, terça-feira, quarta-feira, quinta-feira, sexta-feira and sábado. They are written with a hyphen and the word-feira, and they are not the same as the European Portuguese forms you may find online. Here is how they work, where they come from, and how Brazilians actually say them.

By Renata · November 16, 2026 · 5 min

The full list, with pronunciation and gender

Every weekday from Monday to Friday ends in -feira. Sunday and Saturday do not. The word feira historically meant a market or fair day, and the full names are still the standard written forms in Brazil. In speech, Brazilians often drop the -feira and just say segunda, terça, quarta, quinta, sexta.

All days of the week are masculine, so you say o domingo, o sábado, a segunda-feira (the word feira is feminine, so the compound is feminine). This matters for articles and adjectives: um domingo tranquilo, uma sexta-feira longa.

Why Brazilian Portuguese looks different from European Portuguese

In Portugal, the weekdays are segunda-feira, terça-feira, quarta-feira, quinta-feira and sexta-feira too, but they are often written without the hyphen and pronounced differently. More importantly, the everyday short forms and the prepositions used with them differ. A generic answer online may give you European Portuguese patterns that sound wrong or unnatural in Brazil.

For example, in Brazil you say na segunda-feira (on Monday) and no domingo (on Sunday). In European Portuguese you may see à segunda-feira. Brazilian speakers also use a gente vai instead of vamos more often in speech. If a phrase feels stiff, it is probably European.

  • Use na for feminine weekdays: na segunda, na terça, na quarta, na quinta, na sexta.
  • Use no for masculine days: no domingo, no sábado.
  • Do not translate 'on Monday' as em a segunda, that is not Brazilian Portuguese.

How Brazilians actually say the days in conversation

In casual speech, the short forms are the norm. You will hear 'Te vejo na segunda' (See you on Monday) far more often than the full segunda-feira. The full form appears in writing, in formal contexts, and when clarity matters. Both are correct; the short form is not slang.

Brazilians also use the days to talk about habits and plans. 'Toda quarta eu tenho aula' means 'Every Wednesday I have class.' 'Na sexta passada' means 'last Friday.' Notice that the article changes with the preposition: na quarta, but no sábado.

A short history: why -feira?

The word feira comes from the Latin feria, meaning a festival or rest day. In the Christian calendar, the days of Holy Week were numbered: segunda-feira was the second feria, terça-feira the third, and so on. Sunday was the Lord's day, domingo, from the Latin dominicus.

Saturday, sábado, comes from the Hebrew shabbat, the Sabbath. This system is old and stable in Portuguese. It is also why the days are numbered rather than named after planets or gods, unlike English. That makes them easier to learn: once you know the numbers, you know the order.

Common mistakes English speakers make

The most common mistake is using the European Portuguese preposition à with weekdays. In Brazil, you say na segunda, not à segunda. Another is forgetting the hyphen in writing: the standard Brazilian spelling is segunda-feira, with a hyphen. Without it, it looks careless in formal writing.

A third mistake is treating domingo and sábado as feminine because they end in -o and -ado. They are masculine: o domingo, o sábado. Finally, do not say 'segunda-feira' when you mean 'the second' in a list; context usually makes it clear, but 'o segundo' is the ordinal number.

  • Say na segunda, not à segunda.
  • Write segunda-feira with a hyphen in careful writing.
  • Use o domingo and o sábado, not a domingo or a sábado.
  • For 'every Monday', say toda segunda, not cada segunda in everyday speech.

Common questions

What are the days of the week in Brazilian Portuguese?

Domingo, segunda-feira, terça-feira, quarta-feira, quinta-feira, sexta-feira and sábado. In speech, Brazilians usually shorten Monday to Friday to segunda, terça, quarta, quinta and sexta. The full forms are standard in writing.

Are Brazilian and European Portuguese weekdays the same?

The names are similar, but the everyday usage and prepositions differ. In Brazil you say na segunda-feira; in Portugal you may see à segunda-feira. A generic answer online can give you European patterns that sound unnatural in Brazil.

Why do the days end in -feira?

Feira comes from the Latin feria, a festival or rest day. In the Christian calendar, the days of Holy Week were numbered, so Monday was the second feria, Tuesday the third, and so on. Sunday is domingo, the Lord's day, and Saturday is sábado, from the Sabbath.

Do I write segunda-feira with a hyphen?

Yes, the standard Brazilian spelling uses a hyphen: segunda-feira, terça-feira, and so on. Without the hyphen it looks careless in formal writing. In casual messages, many people just write segunda.

Is domingo masculine or feminine?

Domingo is masculine: o domingo. Sábado is also masculine: o sábado. The weekdays from Monday to Friday are feminine because feira is feminine: a segunda-feira. So you say no domingo but na segunda-feira.
