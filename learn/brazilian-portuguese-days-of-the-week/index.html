Brazilian Portuguese Basics

What Are the Brazilian Portuguese Days of the Week?

The Brazilian Portuguese days of the week are: segunda-feira (Monday), terça-feira (Tuesday), quarta-feira (Wednesday), quinta-feira (Thursday), sexta-feira (Friday), sábado (Saturday) and domingo (Sunday). They are written with a hyphen and an accent on terça and sábado. This guide explains the forms, the pronunciation and the mistakes English speakers make.

By Renata · October 27, 2026 · 4 min

The full list with correct spelling and accents

Brazilian Portuguese uses a simple pattern for Monday to Friday: a number word plus feira. Segunda-feira, terça-feira, quarta-feira, quinta-feira and sexta-feira literally mean second, third, fourth, fifth and sixth fair or market day. Saturday and Sunday break the pattern: sábado and domingo.

The accents matter. Terça has a cedilla (ç) and sábado has an acute accent on the first a. Without them the words are misspelled. In everyday speech Brazilians often drop the feira: you will hear 'na segunda' or 'na sexta' meaning 'on Monday' or 'on Friday'.

  • segunda-feira = Monday
  • terça-feira = Tuesday
  • quarta-feira = Wednesday
  • quinta-feira = Thursday
  • sexta-feira = Friday
  • sábado = Saturday
  • domingo = Sunday

How to say 'on Monday', 'every Friday' and 'next week'

To say 'on' a day, use na for most days and no for sábado and domingo: na segunda-feira, na terça-feira, no sábado, no domingo. For repeated actions, use the plural article: às segundas-feiras (on Mondays), aos sábados (on Saturdays).

For 'next Monday' say na próxima segunda-feira or na segunda que vem. For 'last Friday' say na sexta passada. The word feira can be omitted in all of these when the context is clear.

European Portuguese vs Brazilian Portuguese: what changes

The names of the days are the same in European and Brazilian Portuguese, but usage differs. In Portugal, segunda-feira is often shortened to segunda, and the hyphen is standard in both countries. The bigger difference is pronunciation and the surrounding grammar, not the day names themselves.

If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, do not copy European Portuguese word order or pronunciation from generic online lists. For example, Brazilians say 'na segunda' more often than 'à segunda', which is common in Portugal. Common mistakes English speakers make

The most common mistake is forgetting the hyphen in segunda-feira. Another is using na with sábado and domingo: it should be no sábado and no domingo. Also, do not translate 'Wednesday' as 'quarta' alone in writing without feira unless the context is very clear.

A second mistake is pronouncing terça with a hard c. The ç sounds like ss in 'sun'. Sábado is stressed on the first syllable: SÁ-ba-do. Domingo is stressed on the second: do-MIN-go.

  • Write segunda-feira, not segunda feira.
  • Use no sábado and no domingo, not na.
  • Pronounce terça as 'TER-sa'.
  • Stress sábado on the first syllable.

A short dialogue using the days

Here is a simple Brazilian Portuguese dialogue. Notice the use of na and no, and the omission of feira in speech.

Você trabalha na segunda? Não, na segunda eu descanso. Trabalho de terça a sexta. E no sábado? No sábado eu trabalho de manhã. Domingo é meu dia livre.

Common questions

What are the days of the week in Brazilian Portuguese?

They are segunda-feira, terça-feira, quarta-feira, quinta-feira, sexta-feira, sábado and domingo. Monday to Friday follow the pattern of a number plus feira. Saturday and Sunday are sábado and domingo.

Do Brazilians say 'segunda' or 'segunda-feira'?

Both are correct. In conversation, Brazilians often shorten to segunda, terça, quarta, quinta and sexta. In writing, especially formal writing, the full form with feira is more common.

Is it 'na sábado' or 'no sábado'?

It is no sábado, because sábado is masculine. For domingo, also use no domingo. For Monday to Friday, use na: na segunda-feira, na terça-feira, and so on.

Are the days of the week the same in European and Brazilian Portuguese?

The names are the same, but usage and pronunciation differ. Brazilians tend to say na segunda where Portuguese speakers might say à segunda. If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, learn the Brazilian patterns.

How do you pronounce terça-feira and sábado?

Terça sounds like 'TER-sa', with the ç like ss in 'sun'. Sábado is stressed on the first syllable: 'SÁ-ba-do'. Domingo is stressed on the second: 'do-MIN-go'.
