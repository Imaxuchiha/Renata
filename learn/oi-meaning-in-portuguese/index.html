What does oi mean in Portuguese?

Oi means hi in Brazilian Portuguese. It is the everyday greeting you hear in Brazil when someone picks up the phone, walks into a room, or starts a WhatsApp message. It is friendly, neutral and extremely common. Olá also means hello, but it sounds more formal and appears far less in daily Brazilian speech.

By Renata · October 11, 2026 · 5 min

Oi is the default hello in Brazil

If you say oi to a Brazilian, they will understand immediately. It works with friends, family, coworkers, shopkeepers and strangers your own age. Tone does the rest: a short oi can be casual, a warm oi can be welcoming, and oi, tudo bem? opens a conversation properly.

European Portuguese speakers also know the word, but in Portugal you hear olá, bom dia and boa tarde much more often in everyday situations. If a course teaches you to greet everyone with olá, that is not wrong, but it will sound slightly stiff in Brazil.

  • Oi, hi, the standard everyday greeting in Brazil
  • Oi, tudo bem? hi, how are you? the most common opener
  • Oi, tudo bom? same idea, very common in São Paulo and the South
  • Olá, hello, correct but more formal or written
  • E aí? what's up, casual, between friends

How to answer when someone says oi

The natural reply is oi back, usually followed by tudo bem? or tudo bom? You do not need a long answer. Brazilians often reply tudo bem even when things are not perfect, because the question works as a greeting, not a real request for information.

On the phone, Brazilians often say alô when answering, not oi. Alô is specifically the phone greeting. Oi comes right after, once the conversation starts. In person, alô sounds odd unless you are joking.

Oi is not the same as oi in European Portuguese

This is where many learners get confused. Oi exists in Portugal too, but it is more common as an attention-getter meaning hey or excuse me, and it can sound abrupt. In Brazil it is a full greeting. Same three letters, different job.

The difference matters if you are studying for a European Portuguese exam. The CIPLE exam tests European Portuguese and marks Brazilian usage as wrong, even when the Brazilian form is perfectly correct in Brazil. That is why I teach Brazilian Portuguese only and do not prepare anyone for CIPLE.

Other meanings of oi you will actually hear

Oi can also mean hey when someone wants your attention, and it can express surprise, as in Oi? when you did not understand or cannot believe something. Context and intonation decide which one it is.

There is also the expression dar um oi, which means to say a quick hello, often by message. And in some regions you will hear oi followed by the person's name as a soft way to start: Oi, Ana, tudo bem?

  • Oi! hi, greeting
  • Oi? hey? or what? when surprised or confused
  • Dar um oi, to say a quick hello
  • Oi, gente! hi, everyone, common in videos and groups
  • Oi, sumido! hey, stranger, to someone you have not seen in a while

Common mistakes English speakers make with oi

The biggest one is pronouncing it like the English word oil. In Brazilian Portuguese, oi is a single clean syllable: oh-ee, said quickly, with the stress on the o. It should not rhyme with boy or toy.

The second mistake is using oi in writing where Brazilians would switch to olá or bom dia. In a job application or a formal email, oi can feel too casual. Match the register: oi with people you know, olá or bom dia with people you do not.

Common questions

Does oi mean hi or hello in Portuguese?

Oi means hi. It is the everyday Brazilian greeting used with friends, family, coworkers and strangers your own age. Olá also means hello, but it is more formal and appears less in daily Brazilian speech.

Is oi rude or informal?

Oi is not rude. It is neutral and friendly in Brazil. It can feel too casual in very formal writing or with someone much older in a professional setting, where bom dia or boa tarde fits better.

What is the difference between oi and olá?

Both mean hello, but oi is the normal spoken greeting in Brazil and olá sounds more formal or written. In European Portuguese, olá is the standard everyday greeting and oi is often used as hey to get attention.

How do you pronounce oi in Brazilian Portuguese?

Say it as one quick syllable, oh-ee, with the stress on the o. It should not sound like the English word oil. Keep it short and clean, without adding an extra vowel at the end.

What should I say back when someone says oi?

Say oi back, then add tudo bem? or tudo bom? The usual reply is tudo bem, even if things are not perfect, because the question works as a greeting rather than a real request for information.

Can I use oi in Portugal?

You can, and people will understand you, but in Portugal oi often works as hey or excuse me rather than as a full greeting. If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, use oi in Brazil and expect olá, bom dia or boa tarde in Portugal.
