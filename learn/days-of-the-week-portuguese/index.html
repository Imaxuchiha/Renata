What Are the Days of the Week in Brazilian Portuguese?

The days of the week in Brazilian Portuguese are domingo, segunda-feira, terça-feira, quarta-feira, quinta-feira, sexta-feira and sábado. They are all masculine, so you say o domingo and o sábado, and the numbered weekdays are built from ordinal numbers plus feira. That structure is the first thing English speakers notice, and it is also where most online lists quietly give you European Portuguese instead.

By Renata · November 24, 2026 · 5 min

The seven days, spelled correctly

Brazilian Portuguese counts most weekdays instead of naming them after planets or gods. Segunda-feira literally means second fair, terça-feira third fair, and so on through sexta-feira. Sunday and Saturday are the exceptions: domingo and sábado stand alone with no feira.

All seven are masculine nouns. You say o domingo, o sábado, and os dias úteis for weekdays. The hyphen in segunda-feira is not optional in writing, and the accent on sábado is required.

Why the week starts on domingo in Brazil

In Brazil the week begins on Sunday, both on calendars and in how people talk about the weekend. So segunda-feira is the first working day, not the second day of the week. If you translate Monday as the second day, you will confuse yourself in conversation.

This matters for scheduling. When a Brazilian says na segunda, they mean this coming Monday or the Monday of the week being discussed, depending on context. Ask o dia exato if a date matters, especially for appointments.

  • Calendar week: domingo first, sábado last.
  • Working week: segunda-feira to sexta-feira.
  • Weekend: o fim de semana, meaning sábado and domingo.

Everyday phrases with the days

Brazilians rarely say the full feira form in casual speech. You will hear segunda, terça, quarta, quinta and sexta on their own, and everyone understands. The full form is correct and common in writing, in schedules and in formal contexts.

To say on a day, use na for the feira days and no for domingo and sábado, because of gender and the preposition contraction. So na segunda-feira but no domingo. This small detail is where learners get caught.

Brazilian versus European Portuguese: the trap

European Portuguese also uses segunda-feira and so on, but the everyday pronunciation and the surrounding phrases differ, and some online lists mix in European forms without warning. If a page teaches you to say things in a Lisbon rhythm while labelling it Brazilian, you will sound off in Brazil.

I teach Brazilian Portuguese only, and I do not prepare anyone for the CIPLE exam. That exam tests European Portuguese and marks Brazilian usage wrong, so preparing for it with Brazilian material is a waste of your time. If your goal is Brazil, the days above are what you need.

How to practise the days without memorising lists

Put the days into your own week. Say what you do on each one: na segunda eu trabalho, na quarta eu estudo português, no sábado eu descanso. Short sentences about your real routine stick far better than reciting the list.

Then practise the prepositions separately, because na segunda and no domingo are the part learners forget under pressure. Say them out loud until the contraction feels automatic. That is the difference between knowing the names and being able to use them.

  • Write your week in Portuguese, one line per day.
  • Say the day plus one activity out loud.
  • Check the preposition: na for feira days, no for domingo and sábado.

Common questions

Is segunda-feira Monday or Tuesday?

Segunda-feira is Monday. Segunda comes from segundo, meaning second, and in the Brazilian week Sunday is first, so Monday is the second day. Terça-feira is Tuesday, from terceiro, third.

Do Brazilians say segunda or segunda-feira?

Both. In casual speech you will hear na segunda, na terça and so on, without feira. The full form segunda-feira is correct and common in writing, schedules and formal contexts. Use the full form when you are unsure.

Why is it no domingo but na segunda-feira?

Domingo and sábado are masculine nouns without feira, so the preposition contracts to no. The feira days take na because the phrase is treated as feminine. So you say no domingo, no sábado, but na segunda-feira and na sexta-feira.

Does the week start on Sunday or Monday in Brazil?

In Brazil the week starts on Sunday, on calendars and in everyday speech. Segunda-feira is the first working day. This is different from some European conventions, so check dates carefully when scheduling.

Are the days of the week the same in European Portuguese?

The names are essentially the same, but everyday pronunciation and surrounding phrases differ, and some online lists mix European forms into Brazilian material. If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, learn the Brazilian rhythm and prepositions. I teach Brazilian Portuguese only and do not prepare students for the CIPLE exam, which tests European Portuguese.

How do I say every Monday or next Friday?

Every Monday is toda segunda or toda segunda-feira. Next Friday is na sexta que vem, and last Monday is na segunda passada. Keep the preposition: na for the feira days, no for domingo and sábado.
