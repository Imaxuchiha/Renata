Brazilian Portuguese basics

What are the days of the week in Brazilian Portuguese?

The days of the week in Brazilian Portuguese are domingo, segunda-feira, terça-feira, quarta-feira, quinta-feira, sexta-feira and sábado. Sunday and Saturday are short and irregular; Monday to Friday are built on the word feira. Brazilians also shorten them constantly in speech, and that is where learners get confused.

By Renata · November 5, 2026 · 5 min

The full list, spelled correctly

Here is the complete set with the accents you need. Note that domingo and sábado have no feira, and that sábado carries an acute accent on the first a. The hyphen in segunda-feira and the others is part of the standard spelling in Brazil.

All seven are masculine, so you say o domingo, o sábado, os dias da semana. When you mean on a particular day, Brazilian Portuguese normally uses no preposition with most days in casual speech, but the safe pattern is na segunda, no sábado.

  • domingo, Sunday
  • segunda-feira, Monday
  • terça-feira, Tuesday
  • quarta-feira, Wednesday
  • quinta-feira, Thursday
  • sexta-feira, Friday
  • sábado, Saturday

Why Monday to Friday end in -feira

Feira means market or fair. In older Portuguese, the church calendar named the weekdays after the Easter week, and each day was a fair day. That is why you get segunda-feira, literally second fair, and so on through sexta-feira.

This is not a quirk you need to memorise as a story. What matters is that the -feira part is optional in speech but expected in careful writing. If you write segunda feira without the hyphen in a formal text, it looks careless in Brazil.

How Brazilians actually say them

In conversation, almost nobody says segunda-feira in full. You will hear segunda, terça, quarta, quinta and sexta, and the context makes it clear. Domingo and sábado stay as they are.

The short forms are not slang and they are not rude. They are the normal register for talking about your week, making plans and asking what day it is. Learn the full forms for writing and the short forms for speaking.

  • Hoje é segunda. Today is Monday.
  • A reunião é na quarta. The meeting is on Wednesday.
  • Sexta eu não trabalho. I don't work on Friday.

European Portuguese versus Brazilian Portuguese

This is where generic online answers fail you. In European Portuguese, the weekdays are often written with a capital letter and the -feira forms behave differently in everyday speech. Brazilian Portuguese uses lowercase and the short forms above.

If a page teaches you segunda-feira with a capital S and tells you to say it in full every time, it is probably European Portuguese. That is fine to recognise, but it is not what you will hear in São Paulo or Rio. Using the days in real sentences

The most useful pattern is na plus the day for a recurring or specific day, and no plus the day when you are naming it. Brazilians also say de segunda a sexta for Monday to Friday, which is the standard working week phrase.

Watch the gender agreement. Dias is masculine plural, so you say os dias da semana, and when you describe a day as good you say um bom domingo, not uma boa domingo.

  • De segunda a sexta eu trabalho. Monday to Friday I work.
  • No sábado eu vou à praia. On Saturday I go to the beach.
  • Domingo é dia de descansar. Sunday is a day to rest.
  • Qual é o dia hoje? What day is it today?

Common questions

Is it segunda-feira or just segunda?

Both are correct in Brazilian Portuguese. Segunda-feira is the full written form, and segunda is the normal short form in speech. Use the full form in careful writing and the short form when you are talking about your week.

Do Brazilians capitalise the days of the week?

No. In Brazilian Portuguese, the days of the week are written in lowercase: segunda-feira, terça-feira, sábado, domingo. Capitalised forms such as Segunda-feira are typical of European Portuguese and look wrong in a Brazilian text.

Why is there no feira in domingo and sábado?

The -feira names come from the old market and church calendar for the working week. Sunday and Saturday kept their older names, domingo and sábado, and never took the feira suffix. That is why the list is irregular at both ends.

How do I say Monday to Friday in Brazilian Portuguese?

The natural phrase is de segunda a sexta. You will hear it constantly when people describe a working week or a schedule. Avoid translating word for word from English, because the preposition pattern is different.

Are the days of the week masculine or feminine?

All seven are masculine in Brazilian Portuguese. You say o domingo, o sábado and os dias da semana. This matters for agreement, so um bom domingo is correct and uma boa domingo is not.
