Brazilian Portuguese Basics

How Are You in Brazilian Portuguese?

The most common way to ask 'how are you?' in Brazilian Portuguese is 'Tudo bem?' – and the answer is usually 'Tudo bem!' too. It's a greeting, not a real question about your day. Let's break down the phrases you'll actually hear in Brazil.

By Renata · August 19, 2026 · 4 min

Tudo bem? – The All-Purpose Greeting

In Brazil, 'Tudo bem?' (literally 'all well?') is the standard way to ask how someone is. You'll hear it everywhere – at the bakery, in the office, on the phone. It's friendly, neutral, and works in any situation, formal or casual.

The key to sounding natural is to use it as a greeting, not a deep inquiry. When someone asks 'Tudo bem?', they expect a quick 'Tudo bem!' back, not a detailed report. It's the Brazilian equivalent of 'How's it going?' – the answer is almost always positive.

  • Tudo bem? – How are you? / All good?
  • Tudo bem! – I'm fine! / All good!
  • Tudo bem? Tudo bem! – The classic exchange.

Tudo bom? – The Casual Sibling

'Tudo bom?' is just as common as 'tudo bem?' and means the same thing. The difference is subtle: 'bom' means 'good', while 'bem' means 'well'. Both are interchangeable in everyday speech, though 'tudo bom?' might feel slightly more relaxed.

You can answer 'Tudo bom!' or 'Tudo ótimo!' (everything great). Don't overthink the choice – Brazilians use both without noticing. The important thing is to match the energy: a cheerful 'Tudo bom!' back keeps the conversation flowing.

Como vai? – A Slightly More Formal Option

'Como vai?' (how are you going?) is another way to ask, but it's a bit more formal and less common in everyday Brazil. You might hear it from older people, in professional settings, or in songs. It's not wrong, just less casual.

If someone asks 'Como vai?', you can reply 'Vou bem, obrigado(a)' (I'm going well, thanks) or simply 'Tudo bem'. Note: 'obrigado' is used by men, 'obrigada' by women – that's a Brazilian Portuguese rule you'll want to remember.

Why 'Tudo Bem' Is Both Question and Answer

This is the beauty of Brazilian Portuguese: the same phrase works for both asking and answering. 'Tudo bem?' with a rising intonation is a question. 'Tudo bem.' with a falling intonation is a statement. You can even use it as a greeting and a farewell.

This symmetry makes it easy for beginners – you only need one phrase to handle most social interactions. Just remember to smile and say it with a friendly tone, and you'll fit right in.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

One mistake English speakers make is translating 'how are you?' literally as 'como você está?' – which is correct but sounds stiff. Brazilians rarely say it in casual conversation. Stick to 'tudo bem?' and you'll sound more natural.

Another pitfall: don't answer with 'I'm fine' as 'Eu sou bem' – that's wrong. Use 'Estou bem' (I am well) or simply 'Tudo bem'. Also, remember that 'você' is informal in Brazil; 'tu' is used in some regions but with different verb conjugations – that's a topic for another day.

Common questions

Is 'tudo bem' the same as 'how are you'?

Yes, 'tudo bem?' is the most common way to ask 'how are you?' in Brazilian Portuguese. It literally means 'all well?' and is used as a greeting. You can answer with 'tudo bem!' meaning 'I'm fine'.

What's the difference between 'tudo bem' and 'tudo bom'?

There's no real difference – both mean 'all good' and are used interchangeably. 'Tudo bem' is slightly more common, but 'tudo bom' is equally natural. You can use either to ask or answer.

How do you respond to 'tudo bem'?

The standard response is 'tudo bem!' or 'tudo bom!'. You can also say 'tudo ótimo' (everything great) or 'estou bem' (I'm well). Just keep it short and positive.

Is 'como vai' used in Brazil?

Yes, but it's less common than 'tudo bem'. 'Como vai' is a bit more formal and might sound old-fashioned to younger Brazilians. You'll still hear it, especially from older generations or in professional contexts.

Can I use 'tudo bem' to say goodbye?

Yes, you can say 'tudo bem' or 'tudo de bom' (all the best) as a farewell. It's a friendly way to end a conversation, similar to 'take care' in English.

Why do Brazilians answer 'tudo bem' even if they're not fine?

Because 'tudo bem' is a social greeting, not a real question about your well-being. Brazilians usually answer positively to keep the conversation light. If you're truly unwell, you might say 'mais ou menos' (so-so) or 'não muito bem' (not very well), but it's rare in casual greetings.
