Brazilian Portuguese basics

What does oi mean in Brazil?

Oi means hi in Brazilian Portuguese. It is the everyday, friendly greeting you hear on the street, on the phone and in messages. It is informal but polite, used with friends, family, colleagues and strangers in relaxed settings. If you are learning Portuguese, oi is one of the first words you should feel comfortable saying.

By Renata · October 28, 2026 · 4 min

Oi is the default hello in Brazil

Brazilians use oi constantly. You say it when you meet someone, when you answer the phone, when you start a WhatsApp message. It is short, warm and neutral. It does not sound rude or too casual in most daily situations.

Oi is not slang. It is standard Brazilian Portuguese, just informal. You can use it with a shopkeeper, a colleague or a new acquaintance. For a job interview or a very formal letter, you might choose olá instead, but oi is rarely wrong in speech.

  • Oi, tudo bem? Hi, how are you?
  • Oi, bom dia! Hi, good morning!
  • Oi, meu nome é Renata. Hi, my name is Renata.

Oi vs olá: what is the difference?

Olá also means hello, but it is more formal and less common in everyday Brazilian speech. You see olá in written texts, emails, announcements and sometimes in customer service. In conversation, oi is far more frequent.

In European Portuguese, oi is not the normal greeting. Portuguese people usually say olá or viva. If you learn Portuguese from a European source, you may be told that oi is very informal or even strange. In Brazil, it is completely normal.

How to answer when someone says oi

The most natural reply is oi back, often followed by tudo bem? (all good?). You can also say olá if you want to sound a little more formal, but oi is fine. Brazilians often repeat the greeting before moving to the actual question.

If someone says oi to you, you do not need a long answer. A simple oi, tudo bem? works in almost any casual situation. If you are on the phone, you can say oi, quem fala? (hi, who is speaking?) when you do not recognise the number.

  • Oi, tudo bem? Hi, all good?
  • Oi, tudo bom? Hi, everything fine?
  • Oi, quem fala? Hi, who is speaking?

Common mistakes with oi

Learners sometimes think oi is too informal for all situations. In Brazil, it is safe in most everyday contexts. The bigger mistake is using olá all the time, which can sound stiff or distant in conversation.

Another mistake is pronouncing oi like the English word oil. In Brazilian Portuguese, oi is a clean open vowel followed by a short i. Keep it short and bright. Do not add an extra syllable.

  • Do not use oi in a formal written report; choose olá or a neutral opening.
  • Do not confuse oi with oi? said with rising intonation, which can mean what? or sorry? in some regions.
  • Do not translate oi as goodbye; it only means hello.

Oi in messages and on the phone

In WhatsApp and text messages, oi is extremely common. People often write oi and then wait for a reply before saying what they need. It is a way to open the conversation politely.

On the phone, Brazilians answer with alô, not oi. But when you start a call, you can say oi to the person you are calling. So oi is for greeting, alô is for checking if the line is live.

Common questions

Does oi mean hi in Brazil?

Yes. Oi is the most common informal greeting in Brazilian Portuguese. It means hi and is used in person, on the phone and in messages.

Is oi formal or informal?

Oi is informal but polite. It is fine with friends, family, colleagues and strangers in relaxed situations. For very formal writing, use olá instead.

Can I use oi in Portugal?

In European Portuguese, oi is not the normal greeting. Portuguese people usually say olá or viva. If you use oi in Portugal, it may sound very informal or foreign.

How do I reply to oi?

The simplest reply is oi back, often with tudo bem? (all good?). You can also say olá if you want to be a little more formal.

What is the difference between oi and olá?

Oi is informal and very common in speech. Olá is more formal and appears more in writing or formal contexts. Both mean hello.

Does oi mean goodbye in Brazil?

No. Oi only means hello. Goodbye is tchau or adeus. Do not use oi to say goodbye.
