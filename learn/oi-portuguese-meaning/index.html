Brazilian Portuguese basics

What does oi mean in Portuguese?

Oi means hi in Brazilian Portuguese. It is the everyday greeting you use with friends, family, colleagues and shop staff, and it is almost never wrong in Brazil. It is informal but not rude, and it is the greeting you will hear most often in real conversation.

By Renata · November 7, 2026 · 4 min

Oi is the normal Brazilian hello

Oi is a casual greeting, the equivalent of hi in English. You use it when you meet someone, when you answer the phone, and when you start a message on WhatsApp. It is short, friendly and extremely common across Brazil.

It is not slang and it is not regional. A shopkeeper, a colleague and a close friend can all say oi to you. The tone is light, so it fits almost any everyday situation in Brazil.

  • Oi! Hi!
  • Oi, tudo bem? Hi, how are you?
  • Oi, gente! Hi, everyone!
  • Oi, tudo bom? Hi, all good?

Oi versus olá: what is the difference?

Olá also means hello, but it sounds more formal and a little distant in Brazil. You will see it in writing, in customer service messages and in speeches, but in speech most Brazilians say oi.

If you are unsure, use oi with people you know and olá in very formal contexts. Neither is wrong, but oi will make you sound more natural in everyday Brazilian Portuguese.

How to reply to oi

The simplest reply is oi back, often followed by tudo bem? You can also answer with olá if you want to sound a little more formal, or with e aí? if you are close to the person.

If someone says oi, tudo bem?, the natural answer is tudo bem or tudo bom, and then you can ask the same back. Brazilians often repeat the question to keep the conversation going.

  • Oi! Tudo bem? Hi! How are you?
  • Tudo bem, e você? All good, and you?
  • Tudo bom, obrigado. All good, thanks.
  • E aí? Hey, what's up? (informal)

Oi is not the same as oi in European Portuguese

In Portugal, oi exists but it is much less common and can sound a little odd or overly familiar in some situations. European Portuguese speakers often use olá or bom dia instead, and the whole rhythm of greeting is different.

This matters if you are learning Brazilian Portuguese: do not copy European Portuguese greeting habits from a generic course. In Brazil, oi is the default, and using it correctly is part of sounding natural.

Common mistakes with oi

Learners sometimes think oi is too informal for shops or offices, but in Brazil it is fine almost everywhere. The bigger mistake is using olá all the time, which can sound stiff and distant.

Another common slip is writing oi with an accent, like ói. There is no accent in the standard spelling. Keep it simple: oi.

  • Do not write ói, the correct spelling is oi.
  • Do not use olá with friends if you want to sound natural.
  • Do not translate oi as goodbye, it only means hi.
  • Do not assume European Portuguese greeting rules apply in Brazil.

Common questions

Does oi mean hi or hello?

Oi means hi. It is the everyday informal greeting in Brazilian Portuguese, used with friends, family, colleagues and in shops. Olá also means hello but sounds more formal in Brazil.

Is oi rude in Portuguese?

No, oi is not rude in Brazil. It is informal but friendly and extremely common in everyday situations. It is only out of place in very formal writing or speeches, where olá is safer.

How do you reply to oi?

You can reply with oi back, often followed by tudo bem? A natural answer is tudo bem, e você? or tudo bom, obrigado. Brazilians usually repeat the question to keep the conversation going.

Is oi used in Portugal?

Oi exists in Portugal but is much less common and can sound overly familiar in some situations. European Portuguese speakers often use olá or bom dia instead. If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, use oi as Brazilians do.

What is the difference between oi and olá?

Oi is informal and friendly, used in speech with people you know and in everyday service situations. Olá is more neutral to formal, common in writing and customer service. In Brazil, oi is the natural choice for most conversations.

Can I use oi in a work email in Brazil?

In a quick internal message to a colleague, oi is fine and common. For a first contact or a formal email, olá or bom dia is safer. When in doubt, match the tone of the person writing to you.
