Brazilian Portuguese money talk

How do you talk about prices and money in Brazilian Portuguese?

You say prices in Brazilian Portuguese with reais and centavos, using vírgula for the decimal and the verb custar or ser for the price. For example, R$ 25,50 is vinte e cinco reais e cinquenta centavos. Pix is the instant payment system, so you will hear questions like Você aceita Pix? and answers like Pode mandar o Pix.

By Renata · November 4, 2026 · 6 min

Reais and centavos: the basics

Brazilian money is the real (plural: reais). One real is divided into 100 centavos. The symbol is R$, and it goes before the number: R$ 10,00. In speech, you say dez reais. For amounts with centavos, say the reais first, then e, then the centavos: R$ 3,75 is três reais e setenta e cinco centavos.

Brazilians often shorten centavos to centavos or just centavos in fast speech, but the full form is safer for learners. Note that the comma is the decimal separator in Brazil, not the dot. So R$ 1.234,56 means one thousand two hundred thirty-four reais and fifty-six centavos. The dot separates thousands.

  • R$ 1,00 = um real
  • R$ 2,50 = dois reais e cinquenta centavos
  • R$ 100,00 = cem reais
  • R$ 1.500,00 = mil e quinhentos reais

Saying prices out loud

When you ask the price, use Quanto custa? or Quanto é? Both are common. Quanto custa isso? means How much does that cost? Quanto é? is shorter and equally natural. To answer, you can say Custa dez reais or É dez reais. The verb custar is slightly more formal; é is very common in shops.

For prices with centavos, Brazilians often round or say the centavos quickly. If the price is R$ 12,50, you might hear doze e cinquenta or doze reais e cinquenta centavos. In writing, always use the full form. In speech, context makes it clear. Practice the rhythm: vinte e cinco reais e noventa e nove centavos.

Pix vocabulary: the instant payment

Pix is Brazil's instant payment system, and it is everywhere. You will hear Aceita Pix? (Do you accept Pix?) and Pode mandar o Pix? (Can you send the Pix?). The word Pix is used as a noun: o Pix. To ask for someone's Pix key, say Qual é a sua chave Pix? The key can be a phone number, email, CPF, or random code.

When paying, you might say Vou pagar com Pix (I'll pay with Pix) or Faz um Pix de cinquenta reais? (Can you make a Pix of fifty reais?). The verb pagar is regular. Note that Pix is not a card; it is a transfer. So you will not hear passAR no Pix like a credit card. You say pagar com Pix or fazer um Pix.

  • Aceita Pix? = Do you accept Pix?
  • Qual é a chave Pix? = What is the Pix key?
  • Pode mandar o comprovante? = Can you send the receipt?
  • Já fiz o Pix = I already made the Pix

Brazilian vs European Portuguese: money words

In European Portuguese, the currency is the euro, and the decimal is also a comma. But the vocabulary around money differs. For example, in Brazil you say dinheiro for money, but in Portugal you might hear massa or guita in slang. More importantly, Pix does not exist in Portugal. If you learn European Portuguese, you will not learn Pix vocabulary.

Also, the verb to withdraw money: in Brazil, sacar dinheiro. In Portugal, levantar dinheiro. And the word for change (coins): troco in Brazil, troco in Portugal too, but the verb trocar is used differently. Be careful: a generic phrasebook might teach European Portuguese and mark Brazilian usage as wrong. For Brazilian Portuguese, stick to Brazilian sources.

Common phrases for shopping and paying

In a shop, you might ask: Quanto custa? (How much does it cost?), Tá quanto? (colloquial: How much is it?), or Qual é o preço? (What is the price?). To say it is expensive, use É caro or Tá caro. Cheap is barato. To ask for a discount, say Tem desconto? or Faz um desconto?

When paying, you can say Vou pagar em dinheiro (I'll pay in cash), Vou pagar no cartão (I'll pay by card), or Vou pagar com Pix. If you need a receipt, ask Pode me dar o recibo? or a nota fiscal. In Brazil, nota fiscal is the official receipt.

  • Tá quanto? = How much is it? (colloquial)
  • Tem desconto? = Is there a discount?
  • Vou pagar em dinheiro = I'll pay in cash
  • Pode me dar a nota fiscal? = Can you give me the receipt?

Learning to talk about money naturally

If you want to practice prices and money in Brazilian Portuguese, focus on listening to real Brazilians. Numbers with centavos are the hardest part. Start with simple prices under 100 reais, then move to thousands. Use the comma correctly in writing, and practice saying the full phrase aloud.

Common questions

How do you say prices in Brazilian Portuguese?

Say the reais first, then e, then the centavos. For example, R$ 12,50 is doze reais e cinquenta centavos. Use a comma for decimals, not a dot. The verb custar or ser works: Custa doze reais e cinquenta centavos or É doze e cinquenta.

What is Pix and how do I talk about it in Portuguese?

Pix is Brazil's instant payment system. You ask Aceita Pix? (Do you accept Pix?) and say Vou pagar com Pix (I'll pay with Pix). To ask for the key, say Qual é a chave Pix? The word is masculine: o Pix.

Is Brazilian money vocabulary different from European Portuguese?

Yes, in some words. For example, to withdraw money, Brazil says sacar dinheiro, Portugal says levantar dinheiro. Pix does not exist in Portugal. Also, be careful with generic phrasebooks that teach European Portuguese; they may mark Brazilian usage as wrong.

How do I ask for a discount in Brazilian Portuguese?

Say Tem desconto? (Is there a discount?) or Faz um desconto? (Can you give a discount?). You can also say Tá caro (It's expensive) to hint. These are common in informal shopping.

How do I say 'I'll pay in cash' or 'by card'?

Vou pagar em dinheiro means I'll pay in cash. Vou pagar no cartão means I'll pay by card. For Pix, say Vou pagar com Pix. You can also ask Pode ser no cartão? (Can it be by card?).
