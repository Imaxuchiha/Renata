Brazilian Portuguese basics

Is 'oi' Portuguese? Yes, and here's what it really means

Yes, 'oi' is Portuguese. It is the everyday Brazilian Portuguese word for 'hi'. If you have heard it in Brazil or from a Brazilian friend, that is exactly what it is: a friendly, informal greeting. It is not European Portuguese, and that difference matters if you are learning the language.

By Renata · November 22, 2026 · 4 min

What does 'oi' mean?

'Oi' means 'hi' in Brazilian Portuguese. It is informal, friendly and extremely common. You use it with friends, family, colleagues and people you already know. It is not rude, but it is not the word you would use in a formal email or with a stranger in a very formal setting.

The pronunciation is simple: 'oi' sounds like the 'oy' in 'boy'. That is the whole word. It is one syllable, and Brazilians say it all the time, often followed by 'tudo bem?' (everything okay?). So 'Oi, tudo bem?' is a standard, natural way to start a conversation in Brazil.

  • Oi, tudo bem? Hi, how are you?
  • Oi, gente! Hi, everyone!
  • Oi, sumido! Hi, stranger! (to someone you haven't seen in a while)

Is 'oi' used in Portugal?

In European Portuguese, 'oi' is not the normal greeting. People in Portugal usually say 'olá' (hello) or 'bom dia' (good morning). 'Oi' does exist in Portugal, but it is often used as an interjection meaning 'hey' or 'listen', and it can sound a bit abrupt. It is not the friendly 'hi' that Brazilians mean.

This is a classic example of why you need to know which Portuguese you are learning. If a European Portuguese course teaches you 'olá' as the default greeting, that is correct for Portugal. But if you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, 'oi' is the word you will hear every day. Mixing them up is one of the most common giveaways that someone learned the wrong variety.

How do you reply to 'oi'?

The simplest reply is 'oi' back, often with 'tudo bem?'. That is natural and friendly. If someone says 'oi, tudo bem?', you can answer 'tudo bem, e você?' (everything fine, and you?). That is the standard exchange.

You can also use 'olá' in reply, but it sounds slightly more formal. In everyday Brazilian conversation, 'oi' is the default. If you are in a professional setting, you might switch to 'bom dia' or 'boa tarde' depending on the time of day. But among friends, 'oi' is perfect.

  • Oi! Hi!
  • Oi, tudo bem? Hi, how are you?
  • Tudo bem, e você? Fine, and you?
  • Tudo ótimo! All great!

Is 'oi' the same as 'olá'?

Not exactly. 'Olá' is 'hello' and works in both Brazil and Portugal, but it is more neutral and slightly more formal. 'Oi' is specifically Brazilian, informal and warm. You would use 'oi' with people you know, and 'olá' in a slightly more polite or neutral context.

In Brazil, 'olá' is not wrong, but it can sound a little stiff in casual conversation. Most Brazilians greet friends with 'oi' or 'e aí?' (what's up?). So if you want to sound natural in Brazilian Portuguese, start with 'oi'.

Common mistake: 'oi' is not Spanish

Some learners confuse 'oi' with Spanish 'oye' (hey) or think it is a universal word. It is not. 'Oi' is Portuguese, specifically Brazilian Portuguese. In Spanish, 'hola' is hello, and 'oye' is hey. They are different words with different sounds.

Another mistake is using 'oi' in written formal Portuguese, like an email to a company. In that case, use 'prezados' (dear all) or 'olá' followed by the person's name. 'Oi' is for spoken and informal written communication, like WhatsApp messages to friends.
