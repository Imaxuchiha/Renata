What does oi mean in Portuguese?

Oi means hi in Brazilian Portuguese. It is the most common everyday greeting in Brazil, used in person, on the phone and in messages. It is informal but not rude, and it works with almost anyone. This page explains how to use it naturally, what to avoid, and how it differs from European Portuguese.

By Renata · October 9, 2026 · 5 min

Oi is hi, not hello

Oi is the standard informal greeting in Brazil. You say it when you meet someone, when you answer the phone, and when you start a message. It is short, friendly and neutral. It does not carry a strong emotional charge.

The more formal word is olá. Brazilians understand olá and sometimes write it in emails, but in speech oi is far more common. If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, oi is the word you will hear and use every day.

  • Oi, tudo bem? Hi, how are you?
  • Oi, bom dia! Hi, good morning!
  • Oi, meu nome é Ana. Hi, my name is Ana.

Oi is not the same as olá

Olá is correct in Brazilian Portuguese, but it sounds more formal or written. In a shop in São Paulo or a WhatsApp message to a friend, oi is the natural choice. Olá can feel slightly distant if you use it with people you know well.

In Portugal, oi is much less common. European Portuguese speakers tend to say olá or use other greetings. If you learn oi from a Brazilian and then use it in Lisbon, people will understand you, but it may sound foreign or overly casual.

How to answer oi

When someone says oi to you, you can simply say oi back. That is normal and complete. If you want to be a little warmer, add tudo bem? or tudo bom? Both mean roughly how are you, and both are common in Brazil.

Do not answer with estou bem, obrigado unless you are actually answering a how-are-you question. A simple oi, tudo bem? works as both a greeting and a light check-in. The other person will usually reply tudo bem and then continue.

  • Oi, tudo bem? Hi, how are you?
  • Oi, tudo bom? Hi, all good?
  • Oi, como vai? Hi, how's it going?

Oi in messages and on the phone

In Brazil, oi is the default opener on WhatsApp and in direct messages. It is normal to send just oi and then wait for a reply before writing the real message. This is not considered rude or incomplete.

On the phone, Brazilians often answer with alô, not oi. Alô is the phone-specific hello. Oi is what you say after you realise who is calling, or when you call someone and they pick up. Mixing them up is a small mistake, but it marks you as a learner.

Oi is not rude, but context matters

Oi is informal, but it is not disrespectful. You can use it with friends, family, colleagues, shopkeepers and even strangers in casual situations. In a very formal meeting or a job interview, olá or bom dia may fit better.

If you are unsure, start with oi and add a time-of-day greeting. Oi, bom dia is safe in most Brazilian situations. It shows you are friendly and also polite. That combination works in person, on the phone and in writing.

Brazilian Portuguese vs European Portuguese

This matters because many Portuguese courses online teach European Portuguese and present it as the default. In European Portuguese, oi is not the standard greeting. If you learn oi from a Brazilian source and then take a European exam, you may be marked wrong for using it.

If your goal is Brazil, learn oi and use it confidently. If your goal is Portugal or a European Portuguese exam such as CIPLE, oi is not the right starting point. The two varieties share grammar and vocabulary, but everyday greetings and pronunciation differ.

Common questions

Is oi formal or informal?

Oi is informal. It is the normal everyday greeting in Brazil, used with friends, family, colleagues and strangers in casual situations. In very formal settings, olá or a time-of-day greeting like bom dia may be more appropriate.

Can I use oi in Portugal?

You can, but it is not the standard greeting in European Portuguese. People will understand you, but it may sound Brazilian or overly casual. In Portugal, olá is the safer everyday choice.

What does oi mean in a text message?

In a Brazilian text message, oi simply means hi. It is often sent alone as an opener, and the other person replies oi or oi, tudo bem? before the real conversation starts. It is not rude to send just oi.

Is oi the same as olá?

They both mean hello, but they are not interchangeable in tone. Oi is informal and very common in Brazil. Olá is more formal or written, and it is used in both Brazil and Portugal.

How do I reply to oi?

The simplest reply is oi back. If you want to be warmer, say oi, tudo bem? or oi, tudo bom? Both are natural in Brazil. You do not need a long answer unless the other person asks a real question.

Does oi mean something else in Portuguese?

In Brazilian Portuguese, oi is almost always just hi. It is not a slang insult or a double meaning in everyday speech. The main confusion for learners is that it is informal and not used the same way in Portugal.
