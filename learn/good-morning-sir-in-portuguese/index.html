How do you say good morning sir in Brazilian Portuguese?

The direct translation of good morning sir is Bom dia, senhor. But in Brazil you will rarely hear it said exactly that way, because senhor is heavier and more formal than English speakers expect. Here is what to say, how to pronounce it, and when to leave the sir out.

By Renata · November 11, 2026 · 5 min

The short answer: Bom dia, senhor

Bom dia means good morning and works from sunrise until roughly noon. Senhor is the formal word for sir, used for a man you address with respect: an older person, a customer, an official, someone you do not know well. Put them together and you get Bom dia, senhor.

For a woman the phrase is Bom dia, senhora. Note the spelling: senhora, not senora. The tilde on the o is not optional in Portuguese, and dropping it is the single most common spelling mistake English speakers make with this word.

  • Bom dia, senhor. Good morning, sir. (to a man)
  • Bom dia, senhora. Good morning, ma'am. (to a woman)
  • Bom dia. Good morning. (neutral, works with almost anyone)

How to pronounce it without sounding stiff

Bom dia sounds roughly like bohn DEE-ah, with the m in bom nasal and barely pronounced as a consonant. Senhor sounds like sen-YOR, with the nh making the same sound as the ny in canyon. Say it as one smooth phrase, not three separate words.

Brazilians usually soften the whole thing. In everyday speech you will hear Bom dia, senhor with a friendly rising tone, and often the senhor is shortened in casual talk to seu, as in Bom dia, seu João. That shortened form is conversational and warm, not disrespectful, but it is not what you would write in a formal message.

When Brazilians actually use senhor

This is where Brazilian Portuguese differs from what a dictionary suggests. Brazil is a relatively informal country, and senhor can sound distant or even old-fashioned between people of similar age. Using it with a colleague your own age may come across as oddly stiff.

Use senhor with clear reason: an elderly person, a client, a government clerk, a doctor, a stranger you are asking for help. With a friend, a coworker, a barista or a taxi driver, plain Bom dia is friendlier and more natural. When in doubt, drop the sir. Nobody in Brazil is offended by a simple Bom dia.

Variations you will hear in Brazil

Bom dia, senhor is correct but not the only option. Brazilians often attach the person's name instead of the title: Bom dia, João. This is warmer and very common in workplaces. You can also add tudo bem, meaning all good, to make the greeting conversational: Bom dia, senhor, tudo bem?

One warning about a phrase you may find online. European Portuguese sometimes uses different forms of address, and some sources mix the two. In Brazil, senhor and senhora are the standard formal titles. Do not use você with senhor in the same breath; choose one register and stay in it.

  • Bom dia, senhor, tudo bem? Good morning sir, how are you?
  • Bom dia, seu João. Morning, Mr João. (casual, spoken)
  • Bom dia, doutor. Good morning, doctor. (used for many professionals, not only doctors)

Other times of day, same pattern

The structure carries over. Good afternoon is Boa tarde, and good evening or good night is Boa noite. Add senhor or senhora the same way: Boa tarde, senhora. Boa noite, senhor. Notice that boa is feminine for tarde and noite, while bom is masculine for dia.

Brazilians say Boa noite both when arriving in the evening and when leaving at night, so context does the work. If you are greeting someone at 8pm, Boa noite, senhor is perfectly normal and not a goodbye.

Common questions

Is it Bom dia, senhor or Bom dia, senhora?

Senhor is for a man, senhora is for a woman. The rest of the phrase does not change. If you are unsure of someone's gender or addressing a group, plain Bom dia is always safe and never rude.

Do Brazilians really say senhor in everyday life?

Less than textbooks suggest. It is used for older people, clients, officials and strangers you are asking for help. Between friends, colleagues and service staff, plain Bom dia is more natural. Using senhor with a peer can sound distant.

How do you pronounce senhor?

Roughly sen-YOR, with nh sounding like the ny in canyon. The final r in Brazilian Portuguese is soft, almost like an h at the end of a word in some regions. Keep the stress on the last syllable.

What is the difference between senhor and você?

Senhor is formal and respectful, used with people you do not know well or who are older. Você is the everyday you, used with friends, family and most peers. Do not mix both in one sentence; pick the register that fits the situation.

Is Bom dia, senhor the same in Portugal and Brazil?

The words are the same, but European Portuguese uses different address forms and pronunciation in many situations. If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, learn the Brazilian patterns, because a phrase that is fine in Lisbon can sound off in São Paulo.
