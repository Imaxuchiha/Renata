What does oi mean in Brazil?

Oi is the everyday Brazilian Portuguese word for "hi" or "hello." It is informal, friendly, and used constantly in Brazil among friends, family, and even strangers in casual settings. But it is not the only greeting, and using it in the wrong situation can sound odd. This guide explains what oi means, how to use it naturally, and how it differs from European Portuguese.

By Renata · November 1, 2026 · 5 min

Oi: the basic meaning and pronunciation

Oi is a casual greeting that translates to "hi" or "hello." It is pronounced like "oy" in English, with an open o and a clear i. In Brazil, it is the most common way to greet someone you know or someone your own age in an informal context.

Unlike English "hi," oi can also be used to get someone's attention, similar to "hey" or "excuse me." For example, if you need to ask a stranger a quick question, you might start with Oi, você sabe onde fica a padaria? (Hi, do you know where the bakery is?). It is polite enough for everyday interactions but not for formal ones.

  • Oi, tudo bem? Hi, how are you? (the most common casual greeting)
  • Oi, bom dia! Hi, good morning! (combining oi with a time-of-day greeting)
  • Oi, desculpa, você pode me ajudar? Hi, sorry, can you help me?

When to use oi and when to avoid it

Use oi with friends, family, colleagues you know well, and in relaxed social situations. It is also fine in many shops and cafés when you are greeted casually. However, avoid oi in formal contexts: with bosses, in official meetings, or when speaking to someone much older whom you do not know. In those cases, use bom dia (good morning), boa tarde (good afternoon), or boa noite (good evening).

If you are unsure, start with a time-of-day greeting and wait to see how the other person responds. Brazilians often switch to oi once the conversation becomes friendly. Also, note that oi is not used in written formal communication, such as business emails or official letters.

Oi vs. olá: what is the difference?

Olá also means "hello," but it is more neutral and slightly more formal than oi. You can use olá in most situations, including with people you have just met, without sounding overly casual. Oi is warmer and more intimate. In Brazil, olá can sound a bit stiff in casual conversation, while oi feels natural.

A third option is e aí?, which is very informal and means something like "what's up?" Use it only with friends or people your age. For a safe choice, oi works in most informal situations, and olá is a good fallback when you want to be a little more polite.

How oi differs from European Portuguese

In European Portuguese, oi is also used, but it can sound more like "hey" and is sometimes considered slightly more informal or even a bit abrupt. In Brazil, oi is the default casual greeting and is used more broadly. If you learn Portuguese from a European source, you might be taught that olá is the standard greeting, but in Brazil oi is far more common in daily life.

This is a key point: Brazilian Portuguese and European Portuguese are not identical. A generic online answer might tell you to use olá in all situations, but that would sound unnatural in a Brazilian context. For learners focused on Brazil, oi is essential vocabulary.

Common mistakes with oi

One common mistake is using oi in formal emails or with superiors. In those cases, use bom dia or boa tarde. Another mistake is pronouncing oi like the English "oy" in "boy", the Brazilian oi is shorter and clearer. Also, do not confuse oi with oi? as a question, which can mean "what?" or "excuse me?" depending on intonation.

Finally, remember that oi is just a greeting. It does not mean "goodbye." For goodbye, use tchau (informal) or até logo (see you later).

Common questions

Is oi informal or formal in Brazil?

Oi is informal. Use it with friends, family, and in casual situations. For formal contexts, use bom dia, boa tarde, or boa noite.

Can I use oi with strangers in Brazil?

Yes, in casual settings like shops or cafés, oi is fine. But with older people or in formal situations, a time-of-day greeting is safer.

What is the difference between oi and olá?

Oi is more casual and warm, while olá is neutral and slightly more formal. In Brazil, oi is more common in everyday speech.

Is oi used in European Portuguese?

Yes, but it can sound more informal or abrupt in Portugal. In Brazil, oi is the standard casual greeting.

How do I respond to oi?

You can reply with oi, olá, or a time-of-day greeting like bom dia. If someone says oi, tudo bem?, you can answer tudo bem, e você? (I'm fine, and you?).

Does oi mean goodbye in Brazil?

No, oi only means hello. For goodbye, use tchau (informal) or até logo (see you later).
