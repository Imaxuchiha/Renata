What does oi mean in Brazil?

Oi means hi in Brazilian Portuguese. It is the most common, friendly way to greet someone in Brazil, used on the street, on the phone, and in messages. But it is informal, and using it in the wrong situation can sound odd. Here is what you need to know.

By Renata · October 20, 2026 · 5 min

The basic meaning: hi, hello

Oi is a casual greeting. It works when you meet a friend, call a colleague, or walk into a shop. You can say it on its own, or add a name: Oi, Ana! It is not formal, but it is not rude either. It is simply the everyday hello.

In Brazil, oi is far more common than olá in speech. Olá exists and is correct, but it sounds a little stiff, like saying good day in English. Most Brazilians use oi many times a day without thinking about it.

  • Oi, tudo bem? Hi, how are you?
  • Oi, bom dia! Hi, good morning!
  • Oi, gente! Hi, everyone! (informal, to a group)

Oi vs. olá vs. e aí: which greeting to use

Oi is the default informal hello. Olá is more formal and more written than spoken. E aí is very casual, like hey or what's up, and works best with people you know well. Use oi with strangers, shop staff, and acquaintances; use e aí with friends.

For a more formal situation, such as a job interview or a first email, Brazilians often skip the greeting word and go straight to Bom dia or Boa tarde. That is safer than oi, which can feel too relaxed in a formal context.

When oi can sound wrong

Oi is informal, so it can sound out of place in a very formal setting. If you are writing to a company, meeting a judge, or speaking to someone much older in a formal context, use Bom dia or Boa tarde instead. Brazilians are usually forgiving with foreigners, but it is good to know the difference.

Also, oi is not used to answer the phone in Brazil. Brazilians say Alô. And oi is not a question word; it does not mean what. If you want to say what, the word is o que. So Oi? on the phone means hello?, not what?.

  • On the phone: say Alô, not Oi.
  • In a formal email: start with Bom dia or Prezado(a), not Oi.
  • To ask what: use O que, not Oi.

Oi in European Portuguese: same word, different feel

Oi also exists in European Portuguese, but it is used differently. In Portugal, oi is often a question meaning sorry? or come again?, like when you did not hear someone. It can also be an informal hey. It is not the standard hello the way it is in Brazil.

How to respond to oi

When someone says oi to you, the natural reply is oi back, often followed by tudo bem? (all good?). You can also answer with Oi, tudo bem? and then wait for their answer. If you are the one starting, Oi, tudo bem? is a safe, friendly opener.

If you want to be a little more formal, you can reply with Olá or Bom dia. But among friends, oi is the normal answer. Do not overthink it: a simple oi with a smile works almost everywhere in Brazil.

  • Oi, tudo bem? Hi, how are you?
  • Tudo bem, e você? All good, and you?
  • Oi! Tudo bom? Hi! All good? (very common)

Common questions

Does oi mean hi in Brazil?

Yes. Oi is the most common informal greeting in Brazil, equivalent to hi. It is used in everyday situations, with friends, colleagues, and even shop staff. It is not formal, but it is not rude.

Is oi the same in Portugal?

No. In European Portuguese, oi is often used as a question meaning sorry? or come again?, not as a standard hello. In Brazil, oi is a greeting. This is one of many differences between the two varieties.

Can I use oi in a formal situation in Brazil?

It is better to avoid oi in very formal contexts, such as a job interview or a formal email. Use Bom dia or Boa tarde instead. Oi is informal and can sound too relaxed in those situations.

How do I answer the phone in Brazil?

Say Alô, not Oi. Alô is the standard telephone greeting in Brazil. Oi is used face to face or in messages, but on the phone, Alô is the natural choice.

Does oi mean what in Portuguese?

No. Oi does not mean what. The word for what is o que. Oi is only a greeting. Do not confuse it with the question word.
