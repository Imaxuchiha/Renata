What does oi mean in Portuguese?

Oi in Portuguese means “hi.” It is the everyday, informal greeting you hear constantly in Brazil. It is not slang, not rude, and not regional, it is simply the normal way to say hello among friends, family, colleagues and strangers in casual settings. This page explains what oi means, how to use it naturally, and the mistakes English speakers make with it.

By Renata · October 19, 2026 · 4 min

The direct answer: oi = hi

Oi is a one-syllable greeting that means “hi.” It is used at any time of day and does not change with the hour. You can say Oi! when you meet someone in the morning, afternoon or night. It is the most common informal greeting in Brazilian Portuguese.

It is not a question and does not require a special reply. The natural response is another Oi! or Olá! or, if you want to be a bit more specific, Oi, tudo bem? (Hi, how are you?).

  • Oi! Hi! (informal, universal in Brazil)
  • Oi, tudo bem? Hi, how are you? (very common)
  • Oi, gente! Hi, everyone! (to a group)

Oi vs. olá: what is the difference?

Olá also means “hello,” but it is more formal and less frequent in everyday Brazilian speech. You will see olá in emails, written messages, or when someone wants to sound polite but not stiff. In conversation, oi is far more common.

Using olá with friends can sound slightly distant or theatrical. Using oi in a very formal context, a job interview, a first meeting with a client, can sound too casual. Brazilians usually switch to olá or a title (like Bom dia, senhor) in formal situations.

How Brazilians actually use oi

Oi is extremely flexible. It can open a conversation, get someone's attention, or answer the phone. You will hear it in shops, at work, at school, and at home. It is also common in written messages, often spelled Oi or oi.

One common pattern is Oi, tudo bem? literally “Hi, everything good?”, which works as a full greeting and often expects a short Tudo bem! in return. Another is Oi, [name], used to address someone directly.

  • Oi, tudo bem? Hi, how are you?
  • Oi, Maria! Hi, Maria!
  • Oi, desculpa, Hi, sorry (to get attention)
  • Alô? Hello? (only on the phone)

Common mistakes English speakers make

The biggest mistake is using oi in formal situations. In a business meeting or a first contact with an older person, Brazilians usually prefer Bom dia, Boa tarde, or Olá. Oi is not wrong, but it can feel too familiar.

Another mistake is confusing oi with alô. Alô is only for the phone, it is what you say when you answer a call. Do not use alô face to face. Also, oi does not mean “yes” or “ok”, it is only a greeting.

Oi in European Portuguese vs. Brazilian Portuguese

Oi exists in European Portuguese too, but it is used differently. In Portugal, olá is the standard greeting, and oi can sound more informal or even slightly odd in some regions. In Brazil, oi is the default.

If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, learn oi as your normal greeting. If you are learning European Portuguese, learn olá first. Mixing them can sound unnatural. This page focuses on Brazilian usage because that is what Renata teaches.

Common questions

Does oi mean hi in Portuguese?

Yes. Oi means “hi” in Brazilian Portuguese. It is an informal greeting used at any time of day. It is not slang and not rude, it is the normal way to say hello in casual situations.

Is oi formal or informal?

Oi is informal. Use it with friends, family, colleagues and in casual settings. In formal situations, Brazilians usually prefer Olá, Bom dia, Boa tarde or Boa noite.

What is the difference between oi and olá?

Oi means “hi” and is informal and very common in Brazil. Olá means “hello” and is slightly more formal. In everyday Brazilian conversation, oi is far more frequent.

Can I use oi in Portugal?

Oi exists in European Portuguese, but it is used differently. In Portugal, olá is the standard greeting. If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, use oi. If you are learning European Portuguese, learn olá first.

How do I reply to oi?

You can reply with another Oi! or with Olá! If you want to be more specific, say Oi, tudo bem? and the other person may answer Tudo bem! It is a simple exchange.
