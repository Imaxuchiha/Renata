How do you say sorry in Brazilian Portuguese?

The everyday way to say sorry in Brazilian Portuguese is desculpa. Brazilians also say desculpe, perdão and me perdoa, but they are not interchangeable: each one carries a different weight, and picking the wrong one can sound cold, dramatic or simply foreign. Here is how each one actually lands in Brazil.

By Renata · October 16, 2026 · 5 min

Desculpa: the default, everyday apology

Desculpa is what you will hear most in Brazil. It covers bumping into someone, arriving late, interrupting, forgetting to reply, and most small things that need smoothing over. It is informal but not rude, and it works with friends, family, coworkers and strangers alike.

The verb is desculpar, and desculpa is the imperative tu form. In most of Brazil you will hear desculpa; in some regions and in more formal writing you will see desculpe. Both are correct Brazilian Portuguese, and Brazilians do not treat desculpa as a mistake.

  • Desculpa, não vi você. Sorry, I didn't see you.
  • Desculpa a demora. Sorry for the delay.
  • Desculpa, foi sem querer. Sorry, it was an accident.

Desculpe: the same apology, one notch more careful

Desculpe is the same word in the formal imperative, and it is the safer choice when you are not sure how close you are to the person. It fits a client, a boss, a stranger you have inconvenienced, or any situation where you want to sound measured rather than casual.

In practice, many Brazilians use desculpa and desculpe almost interchangeably, and nobody will correct you. The difference is tone, not meaning. If you are writing an email or speaking to someone older or more senior, desculpe reads slightly more polite.

Perdão: heavier, and often more emotional

Perdão literally means forgiveness, and it weighs more than desculpa. You use it when the mistake is real and you want the other person to know you are not brushing it off. It can be sincere and serious, or it can be slightly dramatic depending on context.

Brazilians also use perdão as a quick interjection, almost like excuse me, when they interrupt or when they did not hear something. In that use it is short and light. The weight depends entirely on tone and situation, so listen for how it is said.

  • Perdão, eu errei. I'm sorry, I was wrong.
  • Perdão, não foi minha intenção. Forgive me, I didn't mean to.
  • Perdão? Não entendi. Sorry? I didn't understand.

Me perdoa: asking for forgiveness directly

Me perdoa is the most personal of the four. It is a direct request: forgive me. You use it with someone close when the situation is genuinely emotional, after an argument, a betrayal of trust, or a mistake that hurt someone. It is not the phrase for bumping into a stranger.

Because it is so direct, me perdoa can sound heavy if you use it for something small. Brazilians often pair it with a reason: Me perdoa, eu não devia ter falado aquilo. Used lightly, it can also be affectionate between couples and close friends.

How heavy is each one? A quick comparison

Think of it as a scale from light to heavy. Desculpa handles almost everything in daily life. Desculpe is the same apology with a bit more polish. Perdão raises the stakes and asks for forgiveness. Me perdoa is the most intimate and the most emotional.

A useful rule for learners: start with desculpa. Move to desculpe when you want to be careful. Save perdão and me perdoa for moments that actually deserve them, because using them too often makes your Portuguese sound theatrical.

DesculpaLightEveryday mistakes, casual settings
DesculpeLight to mediumWork, strangers, written messages
PerdãoMedium to heavyReal mistakes, sincere apologies
Me perdoaHeavy, personalClose relationships, emotional moments

Brazilian Portuguese, not European Portuguese

If you search for how to say sorry in Portuguese, many results give European Portuguese forms and usage. Some of those are not wrong in Portugal, but they are not what Brazilians say, and a Brazilian will notice immediately. Brazilian Portuguese is the variety you are learning if your goal is Brazil.

This matters for exams too. The CIPLE exam tests European Portuguese and marks Brazilian usage wrong, so studying Brazilian Portuguese and sitting that exam are two different projects. Common questions

Is desculpa or desculpe more common in Brazil?

Desculpa is more common in everyday speech across most of Brazil. Desculpe is the same apology in a slightly more formal register and is common in writing, at work, and with people you do not know well. Both are correct Brazilian Portuguese.

When should I use perdão instead of desculpa?

Use perdão when the mistake is real and you want to sound sincere rather than casual. It carries more weight than desculpa. Brazilians also use perdão as a quick excuse me when interrupting or when they did not hear something.

Does me perdoa sound too dramatic?

It can, if you use it for something small. Me perdoa is a direct request for forgiveness and belongs in close relationships and emotional moments. For everyday mistakes, desculpa or desculpe is the natural choice.

Is desculpe European Portuguese and desculpa Brazilian?

No. Both desculpa and desculpe are used in Brazil, and both are used in Portugal. The real differences between Brazilian and European Portuguese show up in other words, verb forms and sentence patterns, not in this pair.

How do I say sorry for being late in Brazilian Portuguese?

The natural phrase is Desculpa a demora or Desculpa pelo atraso. If you want to be a little more careful, say Desculpe a demora. Both are fine, and Brazilians use them constantly.
