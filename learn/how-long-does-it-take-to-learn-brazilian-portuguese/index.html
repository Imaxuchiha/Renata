Honest timeline

How long does it take to learn Brazilian Portuguese?

For an English speaker, expect roughly 200 to 250 hours of focused study to hold a simple conversation, and 600 to 800 hours to reach comfortable, independent fluency. One weekly evening lesson is about 50 hours a year, so it is a real start but not a finish line. Here is the honest breakdown.

By Renata · October 14, 2026 · 5 min

The short answer, in hours

Languages are measured in hours, not months, because months hide how often you actually study. The numbers below assume consistent, active practice: listening, speaking, reading and writing, not just watching videos.

They also assume you are learning Brazilian Portuguese specifically. European Portuguese has different pronunciation and grammar patterns, and mixing them slows you down. If you study the wrong variety, you spend hours unlearning later.

What one weekly evening lesson really gets you

One 60-minute lesson per week is 52 hours a year. That is enough to build a solid A1 and start A2 if you do 20 to 30 minutes of homework most days. Without homework, it is closer to 30 useful hours a year, because you forget between lessons.

So a weekly lesson is not a fast track. It is a steady, sustainable habit. For many adults with jobs and children, that is the only realistic pace, and it works. It just takes two to three years to reach B1, not six months.

  • 1 lesson per week + daily 20 min = A2 in about 18-24 months
  • 1 lesson per week + no homework = A1 in about 12 months
  • 2 lessons per week + daily 30 min = B1 in about 18 months
  • Intensive 5 lessons per week = B1 in about 6-9 months

Why Brazilian Portuguese is not the same as European Portuguese

Most online courses labelled simply Portuguese are European Portuguese. That matters because Brazilian usage is often marked wrong in European exams, and European pronunciation sounds foreign in Brazil. For example, in Brazil you say 'Você vai ao mercado?' while in Portugal you say 'Vais ao mercado?'. Both are correct in their own country, but mixing them confuses everyone.

If your goal is Brazil, learn Brazilian Portuguese from the start. If your goal is a CIPLE exam or life in Portugal, you need European Portuguese, and a Brazilian teacher is not the right fit for that exam.

What speeds you up and what slows you down

The biggest accelerator is speaking from day one, even badly. The biggest brake is waiting until you are 'ready'. You will never feel ready. Start with ten minutes of speaking per lesson and build from there.

Another brake is studying only grammar. Brazilian Portuguese has a lot of grammar, but you need input too: music, series, podcasts, real conversations. Aim for 70 percent practice and 30 percent theory.

  • Speak in every lesson, even if it is painful
  • Listen to Brazilian music and podcasts daily
  • Review vocabulary with spaced repetition
  • Avoid mixing European and Brazilian materials
  • Set a weekly hour target, not a vague goal

A realistic plan for the next 12 months

If you do one weekly lesson and 20 minutes of homework most days, you can expect to finish A1 and start A2 within a year. You will handle greetings, shopping, directions, simple past events and short messages. You will not be fluent, and that is normal.

If you want B1 in a year, you need two lessons a week plus daily practice. That is roughly 150 hours. It is doable, but it is a serious commitment. Be honest with yourself about which plan fits your life.

Common questions

Can I learn Brazilian Portuguese in 3 months?

No, not to a useful level. In 3 months of intensive study, maybe 100 hours, you can reach a strong A1 or early A2. You will manage travel basics and simple conversations, but not real discussions. Anyone promising fluency in 3 months is selling something.

Is Brazilian Portuguese harder than Spanish for English speakers?

Slightly harder, mainly because of pronunciation and nasal vowels. Spanish is more phonetic for English speakers. But the grammar load is similar, and if you already speak Spanish, you have a big head start. Expect 10 to 20 percent more hours than Spanish.

Do I need to learn European Portuguese if I am going to Brazil?

No, and you should not. Brazilian Portuguese is the variety spoken in Brazil. European Portuguese sounds foreign there and uses different everyday words. Learn the variety of the country you actually care about.

How many hours a week should I study?

For steady progress, aim for 3 to 5 hours a week total, including your lesson. That is one lesson plus 20 to 30 minutes on most days. Less than 2 hours a week and you will forget more than you learn.

Can I reach fluency with only one lesson a week?

Eventually, yes, if you do homework and speak outside class. But it will take years, not months. One lesson a week is 52 hours a year. To reach B2 at 600 to 800 hours, that is over a decade. Most people add self-study and real conversation.

What is the fastest way to learn Brazilian Portuguese?

Move to Brazil or create a daily Brazilian environment: music, series, podcasts, and a language partner. Combine that with a teacher who corrects your speaking. Immersion plus feedback is the fastest route, but it still takes hundreds of hours.
