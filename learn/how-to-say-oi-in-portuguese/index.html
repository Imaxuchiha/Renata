Brazilian Portuguese greetings

How do you say oi in Portuguese?

Oi means hi in Brazilian Portuguese. It is the most common informal greeting in Brazil, used with friends, family, colleagues and even strangers in relaxed settings. You pronounce it like 'oy' in English. But there are rules about when to use oi and when to choose something more formal, and this page explains them clearly.

By Renata · November 27, 2026 · 4 min

What oi means and how to pronounce it

Oi is a casual greeting that translates to 'hi' in English. It is used throughout Brazil, in every region, and it is not slang. You can use it when you meet someone, when you answer the phone, or when you enter a shop.

The pronunciation is simple: it sounds like the English word 'oy' with a clear open vowel. In Brazilian Portuguese, the letter o in oi is open, similar to the o in 'oil' but shorter. Do not pronounce it like 'wee' or 'way'.

  • Oi! Hi! (informal, friendly)
  • Oi, tudo bem? Hi, how are you? (very common)
  • Oi, gente! Hi, everyone! (to a group)

When to use oi (and when not to)

Oi is perfect for informal situations: meeting friends, talking to family, greeting coworkers you know well, or starting a conversation with someone of your age or younger. It is also common in casual written messages, like WhatsApp.

Avoid oi in formal contexts, such as a job interview, a first meeting with a much older person, or a conversation with a government official. In those cases, use olá (hello) or a time-based greeting like bom dia (good morning). Using oi with a boss you have just met might seem too familiar.

Oi vs. olá: what is the difference?

Both oi and olá mean 'hi' or 'hello', but oi is more casual. Olá is a safe choice when you are not sure how formal to be. In Brazil, olá is used in emails, in customer service, and in spoken language when you want to be polite but not stiff.

Many learners think olá is the only correct way to say hello, but in everyday Brazilian Portuguese, oi is far more frequent. If you greet a Brazilian friend with olá, it may sound a little distant or formal, though not wrong.

Common mistakes with oi

One common mistake is to use oi in a formal email or with a superior at work. In Brazilian business culture, start with bom dia or boa tarde, and use oi only after the relationship becomes friendly. Another mistake is to pronounce oi with a closed o, which sounds odd.

Also, do not confuse oi with oi? as a question. When you say 'Oi?' with a rising intonation, it means 'What?' or 'Pardon?', like when you did not hear something. Context and intonation make the difference.

Other ways to say hi in Brazilian Portuguese

Beyond oi, Brazilians use several informal greetings. E aí? is very common among friends, especially young people. Tudo bem? can be used as a greeting on its own, meaning 'Everything okay?' or 'How are you?'. Opa! is an informal exclamation used when you arrive or when you agree to something.

For a more neutral greeting, olá works well. And for a time-specific greeting, use bom dia, boa tarde, or boa noite. These are safe in any situation and show respect.

  • E aí? What's up? (very informal)
  • Tudo bem? How are you? (also used as a greeting)
  • Opa! Hey! / Oops! (informal, friendly)
  • Olá, Hello (neutral)

How to practice oi and other greetings

The best way to learn greetings is to hear them in context. Listen to Brazilian podcasts, watch videos of casual conversations, or practice with a native speaker. Repeat oi, olá, bom dia out loud until the pronunciation feels natural.

Common questions

Is oi the same as hi in English?

Yes, oi is the direct equivalent of 'hi' in English. It is informal and used in the same situations: greeting friends, family, or people you know well. For formal situations, use olá or a time-based greeting.

Can I use oi in Portugal?

Oi is understood in Portugal, but it is not the most common greeting there. In European Portuguese, olá is more frequent, and oi can sound very Brazilian. If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, use oi in Brazil; if you are in Portugal, olá is safer.

How do you pronounce oi?

Oi is pronounced like 'oy' in English, with an open o. The sound is similar to the 'oy' in 'boy'. Avoid saying 'wee' or 'way'.

What is the difference between oi and olá?

Oi is informal, while olá is neutral and can be used in both formal and informal contexts. In Brazil, oi is more common in everyday speech, while olá appears more in writing or polite situations.

Is oi used in Brazilian Portuguese only?

Oi is primarily Brazilian Portuguese. In European Portuguese, olá is more common. However, due to Brazilian influence, oi is also understood in Portugal, though it may sound informal or foreign.

What are other common greetings in Brazilian Portuguese?

Common greetings include olá (hello), bom dia (good morning), boa tarde (good afternoon), boa noite (good evening), e aí? (what's up?), and tudo bem? (how are you?). Use oi for informal situations and the others for more formal or time-specific greetings.
