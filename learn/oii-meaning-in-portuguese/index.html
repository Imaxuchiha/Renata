What does oii mean in Portuguese?

Oii is a casual, friendly way to say hi in Brazilian Portuguese. It is an extended spelling of oi, with the extra i showing a drawn-out, warm tone. You use it with friends, family, and people you already know. It is not formal, and it is not European Portuguese.

By Renata · November 13, 2026 · 3 min

Oii is just a warmer, longer oi

Oi means hi in Brazilian Portuguese. When you write oii, you are stretching the sound to sound more affectionate or excited. Think of it like typing hiiii in English. The extra i does not change the meaning, only the tone.

You will see it in texts, on WhatsApp, and in social media comments. It is very common between friends and among younger speakers. In speech, you hear the same stretch as a longer oi, but the spelling oii belongs to informal writing.

  • Oi = hi (neutral, casual)
  • Oii = hiiii (warmer, drawn out)
  • Oiii = even more playful or excited

When to use oii and when not to

Use oii with friends, family, and people you already know well. It fits relaxed situations: texting a friend, greeting someone you are happy to see, or opening a friendly conversation. It is not rude, but it is informal.

Avoid oii in formal writing, with strangers, or in professional contexts. For those, use olá (hello) or bom dia / boa tarde / boa noite (good morning / afternoon / evening). If you are unsure, oi is safer than oii because it is less playful.

Oii is Brazilian, not European

In Portugal, people rarely write oii. European Portuguese tends to use olá or viva for hello, and the stretched spelling is not part of the usual informal style there. So if you learn oii from a Brazilian friend, do not assume it works the same in Lisbon.

This matters because many Portuguese resources online mix the two varieties. Oii is firmly Brazilian. If you are learning European Portuguese, you will sound odd using it. If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, it is a natural, everyday piece of informal writing.

How to reply to oii

You can reply with oi, oii, or olá, depending on how close you are. A simple oi, tudo bem? (hi, how are you?) is friendly and natural. If you want to match the warmth, reply with oii, tudo bem?

If someone sends oii out of the blue, they are usually just saying hi or checking in. There is no hidden meaning. Answer normally and the conversation will flow.

Common variations you will see

Brazilians love stretching vowels in informal writing. You will see oiii, oiiii, and even oi gente (hi everyone). The more i's, the more playful or excited the tone. This is not a grammar rule; it is a style choice.

Other greetings follow the same pattern. Tudo bem? can become tudo bemmm? to sound warmer. This kind of spelling is common in chats and social media, but you should not use it in formal writing.

  • oiii = extra playful
  • oi gente = hi everyone
  • oii, tudo bem? = hi, how are you?

Common questions

Is oii the same as oi?

Yes, oii means the same as oi (hi). The extra i just makes it sound warmer or more drawn out, like hiiii in English. It is informal and friendly.

Is oii used in Portugal?

Not really. Oii is a Brazilian Portuguese informal spelling. In Portugal, people are more likely to write olá or viva. European Portuguese does not usually stretch oi this way.

Can I use oii with my boss?

It depends on your relationship. Oii is informal, so it is fine with a friendly boss in a relaxed workplace. In a formal setting, use olá or bom dia instead.

How do I pronounce oii?

It sounds like a long 'oh-ee' with the stress on the first syllable. The extra i just means you hold the ee sound a little longer. In speech, it is the same as oi but stretched.

What does oiii mean?

Oiii is just a more playful or excited version of oii. The more i's you add, the more enthusiastic or affectionate it sounds. It is common in texts between friends.

Is oii formal or informal?

Oii is informal. Use it with friends, family, and people you know well. For formal situations, use olá, bom dia, boa tarde, or boa noite.
