Brazilian Portuguese basics

What does oi mean in Portuguese?

Oi means "hi" in Brazilian Portuguese. It is the most common, casual greeting you will hear in Brazil, used in person, on the phone and in messages. It is not slang and it is not rude, but it is informal, so you would not open a job interview or a formal letter with it.

By Renata · November 10, 2026 · 4 min

Oi is the default Brazilian hello

In Brazil, oi is what people actually say when they meet a friend, answer the phone or start a WhatsApp message. It is short, friendly and neutral in tone. You can say it once or repeat it: Oi! Oi, tudo bem?

The word has no accent and is pronounced like "oy", rhyming with the English word "boy". It does not change for gender or number. One oi works for one person or a group.

  • Oi, tudo bem? Hi, how are you?
  • Oi, tudo bom? Hi, all good? (very common in speech)
  • Oi, gente! Hi, everyone! (casual, to a group)

Oi versus olá, bom dia and e aí

Olá is also "hello", but in Brazil it sounds more formal or slightly distant. You will see it in emails, customer service scripts and written notices. In everyday conversation, Brazilians usually pick oi instead.

Bom dia, boa tarde and boa noite are time-based greetings and work in both formal and informal situations. E aí is even more casual than oi, close to "hey" or "what's up", and is common among friends and younger speakers.

How to reply to oi

The natural reply is another oi, often followed by tudo bem? You can also answer with oi, tudo bem? and then add a short status: tudo bem, e você? That is the standard back-and-forth.

If someone writes just oi to you, replying with oi, tudo bem? is enough. There is no special rule and no need to match formality, because oi is already informal.

  • Oi, tudo bem? Hi, how are you?
  • Oi, tudo bem, e você? Hi, I'm fine, and you?
  • Oi, tudo bom? Hi, all good?
  • Oi! Hi! (short reply, also fine)

Oi is not the same as tchau

Oi opens a conversation. Tchau closes it, and it means "bye". A common mistake for English speakers is using oi when leaving, because "hi" and "bye" feel symmetrical in English. In Portuguese they are not.

For a more casual goodbye among friends, Brazilians also say falou or até mais. But tchau is the safe, universal choice.

Watch out for European Portuguese

Oi is Brazilian. In Portugal, people say olá or viva far more often, and oi can sound odd or even be understood as a different word in some contexts. If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, oi is correct and natural; if you are learning European Portuguese, it is not the default.

This matters because many online dictionaries mix the two varieties. A generic answer that says oi is "hello in Portuguese" is only half true: it is hello in Brazilian Portuguese, and that is the variety you should be learning if Brazil is your target.

Learning oi in context

One word is easy. The hard part is knowing when to use oi, when to switch to olá, and how to keep the conversation going after the greeting. That is what lessons should focus on: real dialogues, pronunciation and the small phrases that follow.

Common questions

Is oi formal or informal?

Oi is informal. It is perfect with friends, family, colleagues you know well and in everyday messages. For a job interview, a formal email or a first contact with a client, use olá or bom dia instead.

Is oi used in Portugal?

Oi is Brazilian. In Portugal, olá and viva are far more common, and oi can sound out of place. If you are learning European Portuguese, do not treat oi as your default greeting.

What does oi, tudo bem mean?

It means "hi, how are you?" It is the standard Brazilian greeting plus the standard check-in. The usual reply is oi, tudo bem, e você? hi, I'm fine, and you?

Can I say oi to my boss in Brazil?

In many Brazilian workplaces, oi is fine among colleagues, especially in informal teams. In a formal meeting, a first email or a job interview, use olá or bom dia. When in doubt, start formal and switch to oi if the other person does.

Is oi the same as hi in English?

Yes, in tone and use. Oi is the direct Brazilian equivalent of "hi": short, friendly and casual. The difference is that English speakers also use "hi" in semi-formal emails, while Brazilians usually switch to olá there.

How do you pronounce oi?

It sounds like "oy", rhyming with the English word "boy". The o is open and the i is short. There is no accent and no variation for gender or number.
