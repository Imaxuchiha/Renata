Days of the Week in Portuguese: What Brazilians Really Say

In Brazilian Portuguese, the days of the week are segunda-feira, terça-feira, quarta-feira, quinta-feira, sexta-feira, sábado and domingo. They are not the same as in Portugal, where people say segunda, terça and so on. This guide explains the Brazilian forms, how to use them in real sentences, and the mistakes English speakers make.

By Renata · November 25, 2026 · 4 min

The seven days in Brazilian Portuguese

Brazilian Portuguese names the weekdays from Monday to Friday with -feira attached to an ordinal number: segunda-feira, terça-feira, quarta-feira, quinta-feira, sexta-feira. Saturday is sábado and Sunday is domingo. The week starts on Sunday in Brazil, but Monday is the first working day.

In everyday speech, Brazilians often drop -feira and just say segunda, terça, quarta, quinta, sexta. That is fine in conversation, but the full form is safer in writing and when you are not sure. Note that sábado and domingo never take -feira.

  • segunda-feira = Monday
  • terça-feira = Tuesday
  • quarta-feira = Wednesday
  • quinta-feira = Thursday
  • sexta-feira = Friday
  • sábado = Saturday
  • domingo = Sunday

Why Brazilian and European Portuguese differ here

In Portugal, the weekdays are segunda, terça, quarta, quinta, sexta, sábado and domingo, with no -feira. If you learn the European forms and use them in Brazil, you will be understood, but you will sound foreign. Worse, some online resources mix the two and confuse you.

Brazilian Portuguese keeps the -feira because the full names come from liturgical Latin: segunda-feira means second day after the Lord's Day (Sunday). That is why the counting starts on Monday. European Portuguese dropped the -feira in everyday use, but Brazilian Portuguese kept it.

How to use the days in real sentences

To say 'on Monday' or 'on Mondays', use na segunda-feira or na segunda. For the other days, use na terça, na quarta, na quinta, na sexta, no sábado, no domingo. Notice that sábado and domingo take no, not na, because they are masculine.

To ask 'What day is it today?', say Que dia é hoje? The answer might be Hoje é segunda-feira. To say 'next Monday', use na próxima segunda-feira or na segunda que vem. To say 'last Friday', use na sexta passada or na última sexta.

  • Na segunda-feira eu trabalho. = On Monday I work.
  • No sábado vamos à praia. = On Saturday we go to the beach.
  • Domingo é dia de descansar. = Sunday is a day to rest.

Common mistakes English speakers make

The first mistake is using the European forms in Brazil. Saying 'segunda' without -feira is not wrong in conversation, but in writing or formal contexts, use the full form. The second mistake is using em instead of na/no: say na segunda, not em segunda.

Another mistake is translating 'on Sunday' as na domingo. Domingo is masculine, so it is no domingo. Also, remember that Brazilian Portuguese uses lowercase for days of the week, unlike English. So write segunda-feira, not Segunda-feira, unless it starts a sentence.

A quick pronunciation guide

Segunda-feira is pronounced roughly 'seh-GOON-dah FAY-rah'. Terça-feira is 'TEHR-sah FAY-rah'. Quarta-feira is 'KWAR-tah FAY-rah'. Quinta-feira is 'KEEN-tah FAY-rah'. Sexta-feira is 'SESH-tah FAY-rah'. Sábado is 'SAH-bah-doo'. Domingo is 'doh-MEEN-goo'.

The letter r at the end of -feira is pronounced like an h in many Brazilian accents, especially in Rio. So feira can sound like 'FAY-hah'. That is normal and correct in Brazilian Portuguese.

Common questions

What are the days of the week in Brazilian Portuguese?

They are segunda-feira, terça-feira, quarta-feira, quinta-feira, sexta-feira, sábado and domingo. In conversation, Brazilians often shorten the weekdays to segunda, terça, quarta, quinta, sexta.

Is it wrong to say 'segunda' instead of 'segunda-feira' in Brazil?

No, it is not wrong in everyday speech. Brazilians commonly say segunda, terça and so on. But in writing, or when you want to be precise, use the full form with -feira.

Why do Brazilian and European Portuguese differ for weekdays?

Brazilian Portuguese kept the full liturgical names like segunda-feira, while European Portuguese shortened them to segunda, terça, etc. Both are correct in their own countries, but mixing them sounds foreign.

How do I say 'on Monday' in Brazilian Portuguese?

Say na segunda-feira or na segunda. For Saturday and Sunday, use no sábado and no domingo, because those days are masculine.

Do I capitalize days of the week in Brazilian Portuguese?

No, days of the week are lowercase in Brazilian Portuguese, except at the start of a sentence. So write segunda-feira, not Segunda-feira.
