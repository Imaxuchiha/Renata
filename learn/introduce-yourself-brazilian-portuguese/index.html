How Do You Introduce Yourself in Brazilian Portuguese?

To introduce yourself in Brazilian Portuguese, use a short script: Oi, meu nome é [name]. Eu sou [nationality/profession]. Eu moro em [city]. Muito prazer. This works on day one. The main trap is ser vs estar: use sou for who you are and estou for how you are right now.

By Renata · October 4, 2026 · 6 min

Your Day-One Introduction Script

Start with Oi, meu nome é [your name]. This is the most common way to say your name. You can also say Eu me chamo [name], which is slightly more formal but still natural. Then add Muito prazer, which means nice to meet you. If you are a man, you say Muito prazer. If you are a woman, you also say Muito prazer. It does not change with gender.

Next, say where you are from: Eu sou [nationality]. For example, Eu sou americano (if you are a man) or Eu sou americana (if you are a woman). Then say where you live: Eu moro em [city]. If you are just visiting, you can say Estou morando em [city] or Estou visitando [city]. Finally, you can ask the other person: E você? which means And you?

  • Oi, meu nome é [name]. Muito prazer.
  • Eu sou [nationality]. (americano/americana, inglês/inglesa, etc.)
  • Eu moro em [city].
  • E você? (And you?)

The Ser vs Estar Trap

English uses am for both permanent and temporary states. Portuguese splits this into two verbs: ser and estar. Use ser for identity, origin, nationality, profession, and inherent characteristics. Use estar for temporary states, locations, and ongoing actions. For introductions, you will mostly use ser: Eu sou americano, Eu sou professor, Eu sou do Canadá.

But you will use estar for how you are feeling or where you are right now: Estou bem (I am well), Estou cansado (I am tired), Estou em São Paulo (I am in São Paulo). A common mistake is saying Eu estou americano, which sounds wrong. It should be Eu sou americano. Another mistake is using ser for location: Eu sou em casa is incorrect; it should be Eu estou em casa.

Polite Phrases and Questions

After your introduction, you can ask the other person: Qual é o seu nome? (What is your name?) or Como você se chama? (What are you called?). To ask where they are from: De onde você é? To ask where they live: Onde você mora? These are all common in Brazilian Portuguese.

If you want to be more formal, use o senhor (for men) or a senhora (for women) instead of você. For example: Qual é o seu nome? becomes Qual é o nome do senhor? or Qual é o nome da senhora? But in most everyday situations, você is fine.

  • Qual é o seu nome? (What is your name?)
  • De onde você é? (Where are you from?)
  • Onde você mora? (Where do you live?)
  • O que você faz? (What do you do?)

Brazilian vs European Portuguese

If you search for 'introduce yourself in Portuguese' online, you may find European Portuguese phrases that sound wrong in Brazil. For example, in European Portuguese, you might see Chamo-me [name] instead of Eu me chamo [name]. In Brazil, Chamo-me sounds overly formal and is rarely used. Another difference: European Portuguese uses tu more often, while Brazilian Portuguese uses você in most regions.

Also, the pronunciation is different. In Brazil, you will hear 'meu nome é' pronounced as 'meu nome é' with a clear 'é' sound. In Portugal, it might sound more like 'meu nome é' with a closed 'e'. If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, focus on Brazilian sources. Practice and Common Mistakes

Practice your introduction out loud until it feels natural. Record yourself and listen. Common mistakes include forgetting the accent on é (it should be é, not e), using estar instead of ser for nationality, and mixing up genders. For example, a man says Eu sou americano, a woman says Eu sou americana. If you are not sure, ask a native speaker.

Another mistake is saying Eu tenho [age] anos instead of Eu sou [age] anos. In Portuguese, you say Eu tenho 30 anos (I have 30 years). Also, remember that meu nome é is for your name, but meu is for masculine nouns and minha is for feminine nouns. So you say meu nome (masculine) but minha idade (feminine).

  • Use ser for nationality: Eu sou brasileiro.
  • Use estar for location: Eu estou no Brasil.
  • Say Eu tenho [age] anos, not Eu sou [age] anos.
  • Match gender: americano/americana, professor/professora.

Common questions

What is the most common way to introduce yourself in Brazilian Portuguese?

The most common way is: Oi, meu nome é [name]. Muito prazer. Then you can add Eu sou [nationality] and Eu moro em [city]. This is simple and works in most situations.

What is the difference between ser and estar in introductions?

Use ser for permanent or identity information: Eu sou americano, Eu sou professor. Use estar for temporary states or location: Eu estou cansado, Eu estou no Brasil. Mixing them up is a common mistake.

How do I say 'nice to meet you' in Brazilian Portuguese?

The standard phrase is Muito prazer. It does not change with gender. You can also say Prazer em conhecer você, but Muito prazer is more common in everyday speech.

Is it 'Eu sou' or 'Eu estou' for nationality?

Use Eu sou for nationality. For example, Eu sou americano (if you are a man) or Eu sou americana (if you are a woman). Eu estou americano is incorrect.

Can I use 'Chamo-me' in Brazil?

Chamo-me is European Portuguese and sounds very formal in Brazil. Brazilians usually say Eu me chamo [name] or O meu nome é [name]. For a natural Brazilian introduction, avoid Chamo-me.

How do I ask someone's name in Brazilian Portuguese?

You can ask Qual é o seu nome? or Como você se chama? Both are common. To be more formal, use Qual é o nome do senhor? for a man or Qual é o nome da senhora? for a woman.
