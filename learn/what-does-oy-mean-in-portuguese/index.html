Brazilian Portuguese explained

What does 'oy' mean in Portuguese?

In Brazilian Portuguese, 'oy' is almost certainly a misspelling of 'oi', which means 'hi'. It is the most common informal greeting in Brazil. If you saw 'oy' online or in a message, the person likely meant 'oi' and just typed it phonetically or made a typo.

By Renata · October 29, 2026 · 4 min

The real word is 'oi'

The correct spelling in Brazilian Portuguese is 'oi'. It is an informal greeting, equivalent to 'hi' in English. You use it with friends, family, colleagues, and in casual situations. It is not used in formal writing or with people you address as 'senhor' or 'senhora'.

The pronunciation is like 'oy' in English, which is why you might see it written that way by English speakers or in phonetic spellings. But in Portuguese, the spelling is always 'oi'. There is no word 'oy' in Portuguese.

  • Oi, tudo bem? Hi, how are you?
  • Oi, bom dia! Hi, good morning!
  • Oi, meu nome é... Hi, my name is...

How to use 'oi' correctly

'Oi' is informal. Use it with people you know well or in relaxed settings. For formal situations, use 'olá' (hello) or a time-based greeting like 'bom dia' (good morning), 'boa tarde' (good afternoon), or 'boa noite' (good evening/night).

In Brazil, 'oi' is extremely common and friendly. It is not rude, but it is not appropriate for a job interview or a formal email. When in doubt, use 'olá'.

Common variations and related words

You will hear 'oi' combined with other words: 'oi, tudo bem?' (hi, how are you?), 'oi, gente!' (hi, everyone!), 'oi, sumido!' (hi, stranger!). There is also 'oizinho', a cute diminutive, used mostly in playful or affectionate contexts.

Another informal greeting is 'e aí?', which means 'what's up?' and is used among friends. It is not a direct translation of 'oi', but it serves a similar purpose in casual conversation.

  • Oi, tudo bem? Hi, how are you?
  • Oi, gente! Hi, everyone!
  • Oi, sumido! Hi, stranger! (to someone you haven't seen in a while)
  • E aí? What's up?

Why 'oy' is not Portuguese

Portuguese does not use the letter 'y' in native words. The letter 'y' appears only in loanwords and names. So 'oy' is not a Portuguese word. If you see it, it is either a typo for 'oi' or an English phonetic spelling.

In European Portuguese, the greeting is also 'olá' for formal and 'oi' for informal, but the pronunciation and usage can differ slightly. However, 'oi' is used in both Brazil and Portugal, though it is more characteristic of Brazilian Portuguese.

How to respond to 'oi'

When someone says 'oi' to you, you can respond with 'oi' back, or 'olá', or a time-based greeting. If you want to ask how they are, say 'tudo bem?' or 'como vai?'. A common response is 'tudo bem, e você?' (all good, and you?).

If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, practice using 'oi' with friends or language partners. It is one of the first words you will use in real conversations.

  • Oi! Tudo bem? Hi! How are you?
  • Tudo bem, e você? All good, and you?
  • Oi, tudo bom? Hi, everything good? (alternative to 'tudo bem')

Common questions

Is 'oy' a word in Portuguese?

No, 'oy' is not a Portuguese word. The correct spelling for the greeting is 'oi'. Portuguese does not use the letter 'y' in native words, so 'oy' is likely a typo or an English phonetic spelling.

What does 'oi' mean in English?

'Oi' means 'hi' in English. It is an informal greeting used in Brazilian Portuguese.

Is 'oi' used in Portugal?

Yes, 'oi' is also used in Portugal, but it is more characteristic of Brazilian Portuguese. In Portugal, 'olá' is more common for formal situations, and 'oi' is used informally.

How do you pronounce 'oi'?

The pronunciation is similar to 'oy' in English, with an open 'o' and a 'y' sound. It sounds like 'oh-ee' said quickly.

Can I use 'oi' in formal situations?

No, 'oi' is informal. For formal situations, use 'olá', 'bom dia', 'boa tarde', or 'boa noite'.

What is the difference between 'oi' and 'olá'?

'Oi' is informal, used with friends and in casual settings. 'Olá' is more formal and can be used in any situation, including professional ones.
