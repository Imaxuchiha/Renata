How do you order food in Brazilian Portuguese?

You order food in Brazilian Portuguese by using short, direct phrases like “Eu quero…” or “Me vê…” and by knowing the words for the specific place you are in. A boteco, a por quilo buffet and a sit-down restaurant each have their own rhythm. This guide walks you through a real meal in Brazil, from sitting down to paying.

By Renata · October 10, 2026 · 7 min

The basic phrases you will actually use

Brazilian Portuguese is more direct than European Portuguese in restaurants. You do not need long polite formulas. “Eu quero” (I want) and “Me vê” (literally “see me,” meaning “bring me”) are normal and polite in a casual setting. “Por favor” softens anything if you are unsure.

A waiter may ask “Já escolheu?” (Have you chosen?) or “Vai querer beber algo?” (Would you like something to drink?). You can answer with “Sim, quero…” or “Ainda não, um minuto.” Do not be surprised if the waiter brings the drink first and takes the food order a few minutes later. That is normal.

  • Eu quero uma cerveja, por favor. I want a beer, please.
  • Me vê um prato de arroz e feijão. Bring me a plate of rice and beans.
  • Para mim, o frango grelhado. For me, the grilled chicken.
  • Ainda não, um minuto. Not yet, one minute.

At a por quilo restaurant: the real sequence

Por quilo places are everywhere in Brazil. You serve yourself from a buffet, then the cashier weighs your plate and you pay by weight. The phrase “por quilo” means “by the kilo.” You do not order at the table. You get up, take a plate, and choose your food.

When you sit down, a waiter may ask “Vai querer beber algo?” You can say “Uma água, por favor.” After you eat, you take your plate to the scale. The cashier says “Vai pesar?” (Are you going to weigh it?) or simply “Pode pesar.” You put the plate on the scale, they tell you the price, and you pay. Some places give you a card to mark your drinks; you pay everything at the end.

  • É self-service ou por quilo? Is it self-service or by weight?
  • Quanto custa o quilo? How much is the kilo?
  • Pode pesar, por favor. Can you weigh it, please?
  • A sobremesa está incluída? Is dessert included?

At a sit-down restaurant: ordering and asking questions

At a sit-down restaurant, the waiter comes to your table. They may bring a menu and ask “Já sabe o que vai querer?” You can order for yourself with “Para mim, …” and for someone else with “Para ele/ela, …”. If you have questions, use “O que vem nesse prato?” (What comes in this dish?) or “Tem opção sem carne?” (Is there a meat-free option?).

In Brazil, the main dish often comes with rice, beans, farofa and salad. If you do not want something, say “Sem cebola, por favor” (No onion, please) or “Sem pimenta” (No pepper). The waiter may ask “Alguma restrição?” (Any dietary restriction?). Answer honestly. If you are sharing, say “Vamos dividir” (We are going to share).

  • Para mim, a feijoada. For me, the feijoada.
  • O que vem nesse prato? What comes in this dish?
  • Sem cebola, por favor. No onion, please.
  • Vamos dividir. We are going to share.

Drinks, dessert and small requests

Drinks are ordered separately and often arrive first. Common phrases: “Uma caipirinha, por favor” or “Uma lata de refrigerante.” If you want the same again, say “Mais uma, por favor.” For dessert, the waiter may bring a menu or a tray. You can ask “O que tem de sobremesa?” (What do you have for dessert?).

If you need something during the meal, get the waiter's attention with “Por favor” or “Com licença.” Do not shout “Garçom!” loudly; it is considered rude in many places. A raised hand and eye contact works. For extra napkins, say “Mais guardanapos, por favor.” For the bathroom, ask “Onde fica o banheiro?”

  • Mais uma, por favor. One more, please.
  • O que tem de sobremesa? What do you have for dessert?
  • Mais guardanapos, por favor. More napkins, please.
  • Onde fica o banheiro? Where is the bathroom?

Asking for the bill and paying

When you are ready to leave, you ask for the bill. The most common phrase is “A conta, por favor.” You can also say “Pode trazer a conta?” (Can you bring the bill?). In Brazil, the bill often comes with a printed receipt. Check it before paying. Service charge (10%) is usually included, but it is optional. If you want to pay without the service charge, say “Sem os dez por cento, por favor.”

Payment is usually by card or Pix. Cash is accepted but less common now. You can say “Vou pagar com cartão” (I will pay by card) or “Aceita Pix?” (Do you accept Pix?). If you are splitting the bill, say “Vamos dividir a conta.” The waiter may ask “Débito ou crédito?” (Debit or credit?). Answer “Débito” or “Crédito.”

  • A conta, por favor. The bill, please.
  • Pode trazer a conta? Can you bring the bill?
  • Sem os dez por cento, por favor. Without the 10 percent, please.
  • Aceita Pix? Do you accept Pix?

Brazilian Portuguese vs European Portuguese in restaurants

If you learned European Portuguese, some restaurant words will sound wrong in Brazil. For example, in Portugal you ask for “a conta” too, but the rhythm and some phrases differ. In Brazil, “Me vê” is common; in Portugal, it is not used. In Portugal, “Queria…” is more common for ordering, while in Brazil “Eu quero” is fine.

Another difference: in Brazil, “por quilo” is the standard term for buffet by weight. In Portugal, you might hear “a peso” or “self-service” but not “por quilo” in the same way. Also, Brazilian waiters often bring the bill without being asked if the restaurant is busy. In Portugal, you usually have to ask. Knowing these small differences helps you avoid sounding foreign.

  • Brazil: Me vê uma cerveja. / Portugal: Queria uma cerveja.
  • Brazil: por quilo. / Portugal: a peso (less common).
  • Brazil: garçom. / Portugal: empregado de mesa (formal).

Common questions

How do I say “I want to order” in Brazilian Portuguese?

The simplest way is “Eu quero pedir” or just “Eu quero” followed by the dish. For example, “Eu quero uma pizza.” You can also say “Vou querer” (I will want) which is very common when the waiter takes your order. “Me vê” is casual and widely used in Brazil.

What is a por quilo restaurant and how does it work?

A por quilo restaurant is a buffet where you serve yourself and pay by the weight of your plate. You take a plate, choose your food, then take it to the scale. The cashier weighs it and tells you the price. Drinks are usually paid separately with a card or at the end.

How do I ask for the bill in Brazilian Portuguese?

Say “A conta, por favor.” You can also say “Pode trazer a conta?” The waiter may ask “Débito ou crédito?” if you pay by card. If you do not want to pay the 10% service charge, say “Sem os dez por cento, por favor.”

Is “Me vê” rude in Brazil?

No, “Me vê” is not rude in casual restaurants and botecos. It is a common, friendly way to ask for something. In more formal restaurants, “Eu quero” or “Gostaria de” is safer. Adding “por favor” always helps.

What is the difference between “garçom” and “empregado de mesa”?

“Garçom” is the Brazilian Portuguese word for waiter. “Empregado de mesa” is European Portuguese and sounds formal or even odd in Brazil. If you use “empregado de mesa” in a Brazilian restaurant, people will understand but it will sound foreign.

Can I use European Portuguese phrases in a Brazilian restaurant?

You will be understood, but some phrases sound wrong or unnatural. For example, “Queria uma cerveja” is fine in Portugal but in Brazil “Eu quero uma cerveja” is more common. “Me vê” is Brazilian and not used in Portugal. If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, stick to Brazilian usage.
