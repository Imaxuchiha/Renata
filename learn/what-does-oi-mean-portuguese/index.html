What does oi mean in Portuguese?

Oi means hi in Brazilian Portuguese. It is the most common informal greeting in Brazil, used with friends, family, colleagues and even strangers in relaxed situations. It is not a question and it does not mean yes or hello in a formal sense. This page explains what oi means, how to answer it, and why European Portuguese uses different words.

By Renata · November 9, 2026 · 4 min

Oi is the everyday Brazilian hello

Oi is a casual greeting. You say it when you meet someone, when you answer the phone, or when you walk into a shop. It is short, friendly and almost universal in Brazil. The pronunciation is like the English word 'oy'.

It is not formal. In a job interview or a very formal letter, you would use Olá or Bom dia instead. But in daily life, oi is perfectly polite. Brazilians use it with neighbours, colleagues and people they have just met in a casual setting.

  • Oi! Hi!
  • Oi, tudo bem? Hi, how are you?
  • Oi, bom dia! Hi, good morning!
  • Oi, gente! Hi, everyone! (informal, to a group)

How to reply to oi

The simplest reply is oi back. You can also say olá, which is a little more neutral, or respond with a greeting that matches the time of day: bom dia, boa tarde or boa noite. If someone says oi, tudo bem?, you can answer tudo bem or tudo bom, and then ask the same back.

Do not reply with yes. Oi is not a yes/no question. It is just a greeting, so a greeting back is enough. If you are unsure, oi, tudo bem? is always safe and friendly.

Oi is Brazilian, not European Portuguese

In Portugal, people do not usually say oi as a greeting. They say olá or use the time of day. If you learn oi from a generic online source, you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, which is correct for Brazil but sounds foreign in Lisbon.

If you are going to Brazil or speaking with Brazilians, oi is exactly right. If you are preparing for a European Portuguese exam, oi is not the word you need.

Other meanings of oi in Brazilian Portuguese

Oi is almost always a greeting, but it can also be used to get someone's attention, like 'hey' in English. For example, if you do not hear what someone said, you can say Oi? with a rising intonation, meaning 'Sorry?' or 'What?'. This is very common in conversation.

Do not confuse oi with oi in other languages. In Brazilian Portuguese, it is never a yes. The word for yes is sim. And the word for today is hoje, which sounds different.

  • Oi? Sorry? / What? (when you didn't hear)
  • Oi, você! Hey, you! (to get attention)
  • Sim, Yes (not oi)
  • Hoje, Today (not oi)

Common mistakes with oi

English speakers sometimes think oi is informal to the point of being rude. It is not rude in Brazil. It is simply casual. Another mistake is using oi in a formal email. In writing, olá is safer. Also, do not pronounce oi as 'oy' with a strong English accent; keep it short and light.

If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, practice oi with the right intonation. A flat oi can sound abrupt. A friendly, rising oi, often followed by tudo bem?, sounds natural.

Common questions

Is oi formal or informal in Portuguese?

Oi is informal. It is perfect for friends, family, colleagues and casual encounters. In formal situations, use olá or a time-of-day greeting like bom dia.

How do you pronounce oi?

Oi sounds like the English word 'oy', but shorter and lighter. Avoid a heavy English accent. In Brazil, it is a quick, friendly sound.

Can I use oi in Portugal?

In Portugal, people usually say olá or bom dia instead of oi. Oi is typically Brazilian. If you are in Portugal, olá is safer.

What does oi mean when it is a question?

When said with a rising intonation, Oi? means 'Sorry?' or 'What?', you are asking someone to repeat what they said. It is very common in Brazilian Portuguese.

Is oi the same as olá?

They both mean hello, but oi is more casual and olá is slightly more neutral or formal. In Brazil, you can use oi almost anywhere informal.

Does oi mean yes in any language?

Not in Brazilian Portuguese. Yes is sim. Oi is only a greeting or an attention word. Do not confuse it with sim.
