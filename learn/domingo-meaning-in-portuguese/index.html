Brazilian Portuguese word guide

What does domingo mean in Portuguese?

Domingo means Sunday in Portuguese. It is the day of rest at the end of the week, and in Brazilian Portuguese it is a masculine noun: o domingo. You will hear it constantly in schedules, small talk, and phrases like 'domingo de manhã' (Sunday morning) or 'no domingo' (on Sunday).

By Renata · October 30, 2026 · 4 min

The basic meaning and gender of domingo

Domingo is the Portuguese word for Sunday. It comes from Latin and is a masculine noun, so you say o domingo, not a domingo. This matters because many learners assume all days are feminine, but in Brazilian Portuguese only some days follow that pattern.

Unlike English, you do not capitalise days of the week in Portuguese unless they start a sentence. So you write domingo, not Domingo, in the middle of a sentence. The plural is domingos, meaning Sundays.

  • o domingo = Sunday (masculine)
  • os domingos = Sundays
  • no domingo = on Sunday
  • aos domingos = on Sundays (habitual)

How to pronounce domingo in Brazilian Portuguese

In Brazilian Portuguese, domingo is pronounced roughly doh-MEEN-goo. The first o is open like in 'door', the i is nasal, and the final o is reduced to a soft 'oo'. The stress is on the second syllable: do-MIN-go.

Be careful not to pronounce it like the English word 'domino'. The nasal sound in the middle is the key difference. Listen to a native speaker and repeat: domingo, domingo, domingo.

Common phrases with domingo

Domingo appears in many everyday expressions. Some are literal, like domingo de manhã (Sunday morning), and others are idiomatic, like fazer um domingo (to spend a Sunday). Here are the most useful ones for learners.

Note that in Brazil, 'domingo' is often associated with family, football, and churrasco. So you will hear phrases like 'domingo é dia de descanso' (Sunday is a day of rest) and 'domingo de sol' (sunny Sunday).

Domingo in Brazilian culture

In Brazil, domingo is a day for family lunches, church, beach trips, and football. Many Brazilians watch 'Domingão do Faustão' or go to 'feira' (street market) on Sunday morning. The phrase 'domingo é dia de descanso' reflects the slower pace of the day.

If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, knowing how to talk about domingo helps you join small talk. Try saying 'No domingo, eu gosto de...' (On Sunday, I like to...) to practise.

European Portuguese vs Brazilian Portuguese: domingo

The word domingo itself is the same in European and Brazilian Portuguese. However, pronunciation differs: in Portugal, the final o is often pronounced more clearly, while in Brazil it is reduced. Also, some expressions may vary.

If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, avoid mixing in European Portuguese phrases you find online. For example, in Portugal you might hear 'ao domingo' more often, while in Brazil 'no domingo' is more common for a specific Sunday. Our focus here is Brazilian usage.

Common questions

Is domingo masculine or feminine?

Domingo is masculine. You say o domingo. This is different from some other days of the week like segunda-feira (feminine). So use masculine articles and adjectives: 'o domingo foi ótimo' (the Sunday was great).

How do you say 'on Sunday' in Brazilian Portuguese?

For a specific Sunday, say 'no domingo'. For Sundays in general or habitual Sundays, say 'aos domingos'. For example: 'No domingo, vou à praia' (On Sunday, I go to the beach) and 'Aos domingos, eu descanso' (On Sundays, I rest).

What is the plural of domingo?

The plural is domingos. It means Sundays. For example: 'Todos os domingos eu visito minha avó' (Every Sunday I visit my grandmother).

Is domingo the same in European and Brazilian Portuguese?

Yes, the word is the same. But pronunciation and some usage differ. In Brazil, the final o is reduced, and 'no domingo' is common for a specific Sunday. In Portugal, you might hear 'ao domingo' more. If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, focus on Brazilian usage.

What are some common expressions with domingo?

Common expressions include 'domingo de manhã' (Sunday morning), 'domingo à tarde' (Sunday afternoon), 'domingo de Páscoa' (Easter Sunday), and 'fazer um domingo' (to spend a Sunday). Also 'domingo é dia de descanso' (Sunday is a day of rest).
