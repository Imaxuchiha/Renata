Brazilian Portuguese phrases

How do you say I love you in Brazilian Portuguese?

The direct translation of I love you in Brazilian Portuguese is eu te amo. But Brazilians also say eu te adoro and gosto de você, and those are not interchangeable. Each one carries a different weight, and using the wrong one can sound too strong, too casual, or simply strange. This page explains exactly when to use each phrase.

By Renata · September 30, 2026 · 6 min

Eu te amo: the real thing

Eu te amo is the phrase Brazilians use for romantic love, deep family love, and love for a very close friend in a serious moment. It is not casual. If you say it too early in a relationship, it lands with weight and can change the tone of everything. Brazilians do not throw it around the way English speakers sometimes throw around I love you.

The pronunciation is roughly eh chee AH-moo in most of Brazil, though the exact sound of te varies by region. In writing, the spelling is always eu te amo. The pronoun te comes before the verb amo, and that order is fixed in this phrase.

  • Use with a romantic partner, close family, or a lifelong friend in a sincere moment.
  • Avoid using it with casual friends, colleagues, or someone you just started dating.
  • In Brazil, it is common between parents and children, and between siblings.

Eu te adoro: warm, but not a declaration

Eu te adoro means something closer to I adore you or I really like you, and Brazilians use it for friends, cousins, coworkers they genuinely enjoy, and sometimes for a romantic interest before things get serious. It is warm and affectionate, but it does not carry the same commitment as eu te amo. If someone says eu te adoro to you, they are not declaring love in the heavy sense.

This is the phrase that trips up English speakers the most, because adore in English can sound old-fashioned or overly dramatic. In Brazilian Portuguese, adoro is everyday and light. You can say adoro essa música about a song, or eu te adoro about a person, and both sound natural.

Gosto de você: like, not love

Gosto de você means I like you. It is the safe, honest phrase for early dating, new friendships, and situations where you want to show warmth without promising more. If you say gosto de você to a Brazilian, they will understand it as like, not love. There is no ambiguity.

You can strengthen it with muito: eu gosto muito de você, which means I really like you. This is often the step right before eu te amo in a relationship. Brazilians notice the difference between gosto de você and eu te amo, and they will not assume love from the first one.

  • Gosto de você is safe for friends, coworkers, and early dating.
  • Eu gosto muito de você signals stronger interest without the weight of love.
  • Never translate I love you as gosto de você if you mean romantic love, it will sound like you are holding back.

Word order: why Brazilians say amo você too

In Brazilian Portuguese, you will hear both eu te amo and eu amo você. Both are correct in Brazil. The form eu te amo is the classic one and is what most learners should learn first. The form eu amo você is also very common in speech, especially in Brazil, and it puts the emphasis slightly on você.

European Portuguese handles object pronouns differently, and some European Portuguese speakers would say amo-te instead of te amo. That form is not wrong in Portugal, but it is not what Brazilians say. If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, stick with eu te amo and eu amo você. Do not mix in amo-te, because it will sound European, not Brazilian.

The weight of each phrase in real life

Brazilians are affectionate in everyday speech. They say beijos, saudade, and meu amor easily. That warmth can make English speakers think eu te amo is casual too. It is not. When a Brazilian says eu te amo, it is a real statement. When they say eu te adoro, it is warm but lighter. When they say gosto de você, it is honest and often the beginning of something.

If you are not sure which one to use, choose the lighter phrase. You can always move up to eu te amo later. Moving down from eu te amo to gosto de você is much harder, and it can hurt someone. Brazilians read the difference clearly.

Common questions

Is eu te amo the same as I love you in English?

Yes, eu te amo is the direct equivalent of I love you in Brazilian Portuguese. It carries the same weight and is used for romantic love, close family, and deep friendship. It is not casual, so use it when you mean it.

What is the difference between eu te amo and eu te adoro?

Eu te amo means I love you and is serious. Eu te adoro means I adore you or I really like you, and Brazilians use it for friends, cousins, and people they enjoy. It is warm but lighter, and it does not imply romantic commitment.

Can I say gosto de você to mean I love you?

No. Gosto de você means I like you, not I love you. It is the right phrase for early dating or friendship, but if you mean romantic love, use eu te amo. Brazilians will hear the difference clearly.

Do Brazilians say amo você or te amo?

Brazilians say both. Eu te amo is the classic form and the one most learners should learn first. Eu amo você is also very common in Brazil, especially in speech, and it is correct. Both are Brazilian Portuguese.

Is amo-te Brazilian Portuguese?

No. Amo-te is European Portuguese. Brazilians say eu te amo or eu amo você. If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, avoid amo-te, because it will sound European and not Brazilian.

How do I pronounce eu te amo?

In most of Brazil it sounds roughly like eh chee AH-moo, though the te sound varies by region. The written form is always eu te amo. Listening to Brazilian speakers is the best way to get the rhythm right.
