Brazilian Portuguese basics

What does oi mean in Brazilian Portuguese?

Oi simply means hi in Brazilian Portuguese. It is the most common everyday greeting in Brazil, used in person, on the phone and in messages. It is informal but not rude. If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, oi is usually the first word you should feel comfortable saying and hearing.

By Renata · October 1, 2026 · 6 min

Oi is Brazilian, not European Portuguese

In Brazil, oi is the default casual greeting. You say it when you meet a friend, when you walk into a shop, when you answer the phone, and when you start a WhatsApp message. It works with strangers too, as long as the situation is relaxed. In Portugal, people are more likely to say olá, and oi can sound unusual or even a little too Brazilian.

This is one of the clearest differences between Brazilian and European Portuguese. If you learn from a generic course, you may be taught olá as the main greeting and hear almost nothing about oi. That is fine for Portugal, but it will make you sound stiff in Brazil. For Brazilian Portuguese, oi is the natural choice.

  • Oi = hi (casual, very common in Brazil)
  • Olá = hello (more neutral, common in Portugal)
  • E aí? = what's up? (very casual, between friends)

How to pronounce oi correctly

Oi is pronounced like the English word 'oy' in 'boy', but shorter and with a clear open vowel. It is one syllable. Do not say 'oy-ee' or add an extra sound at the end. In Brazilian Portuguese, the word is written with no accent, and the pronunciation is the same across most of Brazil.

When you say oi as a question, your voice goes up at the end. That usually means 'hi, are you there?' or 'hi, can I help you?'. When you say it as a statement, your voice stays flat. That is a simple greeting. Brazilians use both all the time.

How to reply to oi

The simplest reply is oi back. That is completely normal in Brazil. If you want to sound a little warmer, say oi, tudo bem? The other person will usually answer tudo bem, e você? or tudo, e você? This is a friendly exchange, not a real medical question. You do not need to give a detailed answer.

If someone says oi to you on the phone, you can reply oi, quem fala? That means 'hi, who is speaking?'. In a shop, oi, pois não? means 'hi, how can I help you?'. These are useful patterns, but you can start with just oi. Brazilians appreciate the effort even if your accent is not perfect.

  • Oi. = Hi. (simple and correct)
  • Oi, tudo bem? = Hi, how are you?
  • Tudo bem, e você? = I'm fine, and you?
  • Oi, quem fala? = Hi, who is speaking? (phone)

Oi, olá and other greetings compared

Oi is not the only greeting in Brazilian Portuguese. Olá exists and is correct, but it sounds more formal or written. Bom dia, boa tarde and boa noite are time-based greetings and are very common in both formal and informal situations. E aí? is very casual and mostly used between friends or people the same age.

If you are unsure, oi is safe in almost any informal situation in Brazil. In a job interview or a very formal meeting, you might prefer bom dia or boa tarde. But even then, many Brazilians still start with oi to break the ice. The key is to match the energy of the person you are talking to.

Common mistakes English speakers make with oi

The biggest mistake is using oi in Portugal and expecting the same reaction. In Portugal, oi is not the standard greeting, and some people may find it strange. If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, that is fine, but be aware of where you are. Another mistake is pronouncing oi too long, like 'oyee'. Keep it short.

Some learners also confuse oi with tchau. Oi is for meeting, tchau is for leaving. And do not use oi in a formal email to a company. In writing, olá or bom dia is safer. In speech, oi is almost always fine in Brazil.

  • Do not say oi in Portugal and expect it to be the norm.
  • Do not stretch oi into two syllables.
  • Do not use oi in formal written emails.
  • Do not confuse oi (hi) with tchau (bye).

Common questions

What does oi mean in Brazilian Portuguese?

Oi means hi. It is the most common casual greeting in Brazil. You can use it with friends, in shops, on the phone and in messages. It is not rude, but it is informal.

Is oi used in Portugal?

Oi is not the standard greeting in Portugal. People there usually say olá. In Brazil, oi is everywhere. If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, learn oi. If you are going to Portugal, learn olá.

How do I reply to oi?

You can simply say oi back. To be warmer, say oi, tudo bem? The other person will usually answer tudo bem, e você? or tudo, e você? It is a friendly exchange, not a deep question.

Is oi formal or informal?

Oi is informal. It is fine in most everyday situations in Brazil, but for very formal meetings or written emails, use olá, bom dia, boa tarde or boa noite instead.

What is the difference between oi and olá?

Oi is casual and very common in Brazil. Olá is more neutral and slightly more formal, and it is more common in Portugal. Both are correct, but they set a different tone.
