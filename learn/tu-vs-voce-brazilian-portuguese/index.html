Tu vs Você in Brazilian Portuguese: Which One Do Brazilians Actually Use?

In Brazil, você is the default for almost everyone, everywhere. Tu exists but is regional, and even where tu is used, it's usually conjugated like você. This guide explains the regional reality and how to avoid sounding like a textbook.

By Renata · August 20, 2026 · 5 min

The Short Answer: Você Wins in Brazil

If you only remember one thing, remember this: você is the standard second-person pronoun across Brazil. You'll hear it in São Paulo, Rio, Brasília, and most of the country, in both formal and informal situations. It's safe to use você with anyone, from a stranger to a close friend.

Tu is not wrong, but it's regional. Brazilians from the South (like Rio Grande do Sul) and parts of the Northeast (like Pernambuco and Bahia) use tu in everyday speech. In those regions, you'll hear tu naturally, but the verb form that follows is often the same as você's.

  • Você: universal, safe, used in all regions and registers.
  • Tu: regional, common in the South and Northeast, less so elsewhere.
  • Verb conjugation: even with tu, Brazilians often use você's verb form.

The Verb Conjugation Twist: Tu with Você's Verb

Here's the part that confuses learners: in Brazil, when people say tu, they often conjugate the verb as if it were você. For example, instead of the textbook 'tu falas', you'll hear 'tu fala' (you speak). This is called 'tu with third-person verb', and it's widespread in the South and Northeast.

This happens because você already dominates the verb system. Even in regions where tu is the pronoun of choice, the verb form has shifted to match você. So you might hear 'tu vai' instead of 'tu vais', or 'tu quer' instead of 'tu queres'.

Regional Reality: Where Tu Lives in Brazil

In the South, especially in Rio Grande do Sul, tu is the everyday pronoun. You'll hear 'tu é' (you are) and 'tu tem' (you have) on the street, in shops, and among friends. In the Northeast, tu is also common, particularly in states like Pernambuco, Bahia, and Ceará, again with the você-style conjugation.

In the rest of Brazil, including the Southeast and Center-West, você is dominant. If you use tu in São Paulo or Rio, people will understand you, but it will sound foreign or overly formal, like reading from an old grammar book. Stick with você unless you're in a tu region.

  • South: tu is common (e.g., 'tu é legal').
  • Northeast: tu is common (e.g., 'tu vai sair?').
  • Southeast/Center-West: você is the norm.
  • Using tu elsewhere may sound odd or textbook-like.

European Portuguese vs Brazilian: Why the Confusion

If you've studied European Portuguese, you learned that tu is informal and você is formal, with distinct verb conjugations like 'tu falas' and 'você fala'. That system simply doesn't apply in Brazil. Here, você is neutral, and tu is regional, and the verb forms often merge.

This is why online answers can mislead you. Many grammar resources default to European Portuguese, which marks Brazilian usage as wrong. But in Brazil, saying 'tu fala' is perfectly natural in many regions. It's not a mistake; it's just Brazilian Portuguese.

Practical Tips for Learners

Start with você. It's the safest choice, and you'll be understood everywhere in Brazil. If you're planning to spend time in the South or Northeast, learn to recognize tu and use it with the você-style verb, e.g., 'tu quer' instead of 'tu queres'.

Listen to Brazilian music, watch films from different regions, and pay attention to how people actually speak. You'll notice the patterns quickly. And if you're unsure, just use você—no one will be offended, and you'll sound natural in most contexts.

Common questions

Is tu used in Brazil at all?

Yes, but regionally. Tu is common in the South (e.g., Rio Grande do Sul) and parts of the Northeast (e.g., Pernambuco, Bahia). In other regions, você is the standard. Even where tu is used, the verb is often conjugated like você's.

Why do Brazilians say 'tu fala' instead of 'tu falas'?

Because in Brazilian Portuguese, the verb form for tu has largely merged with você's. So you'll hear 'tu fala' (third-person) instead of the European 'tu falas'. This is standard in many Brazilian regions, not a mistake.

Is você formal or informal in Brazil?

Você is neutral in Brazil. It's used in both formal and informal situations, with friends, strangers, and even family. Unlike in European Portuguese, você is not strictly formal; it's the default pronoun.

Can I use tu in São Paulo or Rio?

You can, but it may sound odd or overly formal. People will understand you, but you'll likely sound like you're reading from an old textbook. In the Southeast, você is the natural choice.

How do I know which pronoun to use?

If you're not sure, use você. It works everywhere in Brazil. If you're in the South or Northeast, you can adopt tu, but remember to use the você-style verb (e.g., 'tu vai'). Listen to locals and mimic them.

Does European Portuguese use tu and você differently?

Yes. In European Portuguese, tu is informal and você is formal, with distinct conjugations (e.g., 'tu falas' vs 'você fala'). In Brazil, você is neutral, and tu is regional with merged verb forms. Learning Brazilian Portuguese means unlearning the European rules.
