Brazilian Portuguese basics

What does oi mean in Brazil?

Oi is the most common way to say hello in Brazilian Portuguese. It is informal, friendly, and used constantly in everyday conversation. You will hear it on the street, on the phone, and in messages. This page explains what oi means, when to use it, and how it fits into Brazilian Portuguese as a whole.

By Renata · October 24, 2026 · 3 min

The meaning of oi

Oi is a greeting that means hello or hi. It is used when you meet someone or start a conversation. It is not used when you leave; for goodbye, Brazilians say tchau or até logo.

Oi is informal but not rude. It is perfect with friends, family, colleagues, and even strangers in casual settings. In more formal situations, you might say olá instead, but oi is still very common in Brazil.

  • Oi, tudo bem? Hi, how are you?
  • Oi, bom dia! Hi, good morning!
  • Oi, tudo bom? Hi, all good?

How to use oi naturally

Brazilians often combine oi with a follow-up question like tudo bem? or tudo bom? This is a friendly way to acknowledge the other person. You can also use oi alone, especially when you are in a hurry or just passing by.

When answering the phone, Brazilians say alô, not oi. Oi is for face-to-face or written messages. In written Portuguese, oi is common in informal chats and text messages.

Oi vs. olá: what's the difference?

Both oi and olá mean hello, but oi is more casual. Olá is a bit more neutral and can be used in slightly more formal contexts, like a business email or a first meeting. However, in Brazil, oi is so widespread that it is rarely considered inappropriate.

In European Portuguese, olá is more common and oi is less used. But since you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, focus on oi as your default informal greeting.

Common mistakes with oi

Learners sometimes use oi when leaving, but it is only for greetings. For goodbye, use tchau. Also, don't confuse oi with oi? as a question; it is not used that way.

Another mistake is using oi in very formal writing, like a cover letter. In those cases, use Prezado or Prezada. But in everyday conversation, oi is safe.

  • Don't say oi when you leave, use tchau.
  • Don't use oi in formal letters, use Prezado(a).
  • Don't answer the phone with oi, use alô.

Common questions

Is oi formal or informal?

Oi is informal. It is used with friends, family, and in casual situations. For more formal contexts, use olá or bom dia.

Can I use oi in Portugal?

Oi is understood in Portugal, but it is less common. In European Portuguese, olá is more typical. Since you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, oi is the natural choice for Brazil.

What is the difference between oi and olá?

Oi is more casual and friendly, while olá is slightly more formal. Both mean hello, but oi is the everyday greeting in Brazil.

How do I respond to oi?

You can respond with oi back, or with a follow-up like tudo bem? (how are you?). A common reply is Oi, tudo bem? and then answer the question.

Is oi used in written Portuguese?

Yes, oi is common in informal writing, like text messages and social media. In formal writing, use olá or another formal greeting.
