What does oi mean in Brazil?

Oi means hello in Brazilian Portuguese. It is the most common informal greeting in Brazil, used on the street, on the phone, and in messages. It is not a question and it does not mean hi in a formal sense. This page explains what oi means, how to say it, and when to use it naturally.

By Renata · October 18, 2026 · 5 min

The simple answer: oi = hello

Oi is an informal greeting that works any time of day. You say oi when you meet someone, when you answer the phone, or when you start a message. It is neutral and friendly, not slang, and not rude.

It is not the same as good morning or good evening. Those are bom dia and boa tarde or boa noite. Oi simply means hello, and it is the default greeting among friends, colleagues, and strangers in casual situations.

  • Oi, tudo bem? = Hi, how are you?
  • Oi, bom dia! = Hi, good morning! (oi plus a time greeting)
  • Oi! = Hi! (on the phone or in a message)

How to pronounce oi

Oi sounds like the English word oh-ee said quickly, with the stress on the first syllable. The o is open, like in the English word law but shorter. The i is short and clear.

In fast speech, Brazilians often say oi with a slight rise in pitch, which makes it sound friendly. Do not pronounce it like the English word oy, which is closer to the European Portuguese interjection for ouch.

When to use oi and when not to

Use oi with friends, family, coworkers you know, shop staff, and people your own age. It is also fine in most workplaces in Brazil, even with a boss, as long as the tone is casual.

Avoid oi in very formal writing, such as a job application or a letter to a government office. In those cases, use olá or a title like senhor or senhora. In a formal meeting, a simple olá is safer.

  • Safe: friends, family, colleagues, casual service situations
  • Safe: phone calls and WhatsApp messages
  • Avoid: formal letters, official emails, very formal meetings

Oi is Brazilian, not European Portuguese

Oi is used in Brazil, but in Portugal it is not the normal greeting. In European Portuguese, olá is the standard hello, and oi can sound strange or even be used as an interjection meaning ouch. If you learn oi from a generic online list, you may be learning a Brazilian word without knowing it.

This matters because many Portuguese courses teach European Portuguese and mark Brazilian usage as wrong. For example, a European Portuguese exam would not accept oi as a greeting. If your goal is Brazilian Portuguese, learn oi as a core word, but be aware that it is not universal across the Portuguese-speaking world.

Common phrases with oi

Oi appears in a few fixed expressions. Oi, tudo bem? is the most common way to say hi and ask how someone is. Oi, tudo bom? is a variation. You will also hear oi, gente! when someone greets a group.

Do not confuse oi with oi? said with a rising question tone. In Brazil, a sharp oi? can mean what? or excuse me? when you did not hear something. Context and intonation make the difference.

How to answer when someone says oi

The natural reply is oi back, or oi, tudo bem? If you want to be a little more formal, say olá. You can also answer with a time greeting: bom dia, boa tarde, or boa noite.

If someone says oi to you and you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, a simple oi, tudo bem? is enough. It shows you understand the greeting and keeps the conversation going.

  • Oi, tudo bem? = Hi, how are you?
  • Olá! = Hello! (slightly more formal)
  • Bom dia! = Good morning! (if it is morning)

Common questions

Does oi mean hi or hello in Brazil?

Oi means both hi and hello. It is an informal greeting used at any time of day. It is not a question and it does not have a special meaning beyond a friendly hello.

Is oi formal or informal?

Oi is informal but polite. You can use it with friends, family, colleagues, and most strangers in casual situations. For very formal writing or official meetings, olá is safer.

Can I use oi in Portugal?

In Portugal, oi is not the normal greeting. European Portuguese speakers usually say olá. Oi may be understood but can sound strange or be taken as an interjection meaning ouch. If you are learning Brazilian Portuguese, use oi in Brazil.

What does oi? with a question mark mean?

When said with a rising tone, oi? can mean what? or excuse me? because you did not hear or understand something. It is not a greeting in that case. Context and intonation make the difference.

How do I reply to oi?

The simplest reply is oi back, or oi, tudo bem? You can also use olá for a slightly more formal tone, or a time greeting like bom dia or boa tarde.

Is oi the same as olá?

They both mean hello, but oi is more informal and more common in everyday Brazilian speech. Olá is a little more formal and is used in both Brazil and Portugal. In Brazil, oi is the default casual greeting.
